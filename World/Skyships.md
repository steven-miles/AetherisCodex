# Skyships

## Purpose

This document defines the role of skyships within Aetherian society. It focuses on what skyships mean to the people of the world rather than player mechanics or ship combat.

---

# More Than Ships

To the people of Aetheris, a skyship is more than a vessel.

It is freedom.

It is opportunity.

It is the promise that there is always something beyond the horizon.

Children watch ships disappear into the distance and wonder where they are going.

Adults watch them return and wonder what stories they bring home.

---

# The Heart of a Skyship

Every skyship is powered by a magical engine.

The engine does not make the ship fly.

Instead, it allows the vessel to catch and ride the invisible currents of the Voidsea.

Without an engine, even the finest ship cannot travel safely.

Magic engines are exceptionally rare, expensive and difficult to manufacture.

Only a single island possesses the knowledge required to create them, though the reasons for this remain closely guarded.

To most people, an engine is simply another part of a ship.

To engineers and enthusiasts, every engine has its own character, history and craftsmanship.

Some crews even give their engines names.

---

# Shipbuilding

A skyship's hull can be built almost anywhere with the necessary skills and resources.

Guild shipyards produce many commercial vessels, while smaller islands often construct ships for local trade and fishing.

The engine is the only component that cannot simply be built anywhere.

This makes every completed skyship a significant investment.

---

# Ships and Society

Most people will never own a skyship.

Guilds fund merchant fleets.

Governments maintain naval forces.

Pirates steal or capture vessels.

Independent captains often spend years saving for older ships.

Many ships remain in the same family for generations, becoming treasured heirlooms as much as working vessels.

---

# Captains

People become captains for many reasons.

Some seek wealth.

Some chase fame.

Most chase adventure.

To command a ship is to choose a life beyond the familiar horizon.

---

# Crew

A typical merchant vessel carries around twenty crew members, each fulfilling specialised roles.

Larger military ships may carry fifty or more.

No successful voyage depends upon a single individual.

Every member of the crew contributes to bringing the ship safely home.

---

# Loving a Ship

Sailors rarely love a ship because it is the fastest or newest.

They love what it represents.

A ship is a home.

A livelihood.

A family.

A promise that tomorrow's horizon may reveal something no one has ever seen before.

Many captains would rather sail a weathered family vessel than command a brand-new flagship.

Sentiment often outweighs practicality.

---

# Naming Ships

Every skyship bears a name chosen by its captain.

Renaming a ship is widely considered bad luck.

The name becomes part of the vessel's identity and reputation, often surviving long after generations of captains have come and gone.

---

# A Sailor's Departure

Before every voyage, a familiar ritual unfolds.

The ship's bell is rung.

The chief engineer places a hand upon the engine housing in silent salute.

Only then does the captain order the anchor raised and the engine awakened to catch the currents.

Whether this ritual truly brings good fortune is impossible to say.

Few experienced sailors are willing to find out what happens if they skip it.

---

# Questions this raises

- Why can only one island manufacture magical engines?
- What materials are required to build an engine?
- How are engineers trained?
- What are the famous shipyards of Aetheris?
- Can engines fail while at sea?
- What is the oldest skyship still sailing?

## What this means for Session 1

Players should see ships treated with affection rather than as simple vehicles.

Dockworkers, engineers and sailors should speak naturally about engines, crews and famous vessels.

The departure ritual should be visible in Port Meridian before the players ever set sail, quietly establishing the traditions of Aetherian sailors.
