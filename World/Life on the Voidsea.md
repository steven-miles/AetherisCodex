# Life on the Voidsea

## Purpose

This document describes the everyday reality of living in Aetheris. It is intended to establish the assumptions shared by ordinary people before detailing any specific locations, factions or campaigns.

---

# The Voidsea

The Voidsea is not an ocean.

It has no surface.

It cannot be sailed by ordinary vessels, nor can it be crossed on foot or by flight alone. Those who fall into the Void do not strike water—they simply disappear into the endless darkness below. Where they ultimately go is unknown.

Every island in Aetheris is suspended above this unknowable abyss.

Despite its terrifying nature, civilisation has endured because people learned to travel across it.

---

# Magical Engines

Skyships do not fly through raw magical power.

Instead, each vessel is fitted with an arcane engine that allows it to interact with the invisible currents flowing through the Voidsea.

Without an engine, a ship cannot catch these currents and would simply drift helplessly before falling into the Void.

The engine provides the means to travel.

The navigator provides the knowledge.

Both are equally essential.

---

# The Currents

Invisible currents weave throughout the Voidsea like rivers through the sky.

Most cannot be seen by the untrained eye. Experienced navigators, however, can sometimes glimpse them under particular lighting conditions or read them through decades of instinct and observation.

These currents form the highways of civilisation.

Generations of sailors have mapped, named and refined reliable routes between islands. While the currents slowly change over time, the principal trade routes remain remarkably stable.

To leave these routes is to leave the known world.

---

# The Trade Routes

Trade routes are the lifeblood of Aetheris.

Every kingdom, guild and nation depends upon them.

Food, timber, ore, knowledge, medicines and crafted goods all flow between islands along these invisible paths.

Their continued existence benefits every civilisation, regardless of politics.

For this reason, the great trade routes are respected almost universally.

Port Meridian exists as a neutral harbour where trade may continue regardless of wars fought elsewhere. Every nation understands that should trade fail, all suffer.

The routes themselves are not maintained by any single organisation.

Rather, they are a shared understanding between generations of navigators whose accumulated knowledge keeps civilisation connected.

---

# Life at Sea

Most voyages along established trade routes are uneventful.

Experienced crews know the currents well, and merchant traffic between major ports is common.

This does not make the Voidsea safe.

Rather, it means that civilisation survives because sailors have learned where it is safest to travel.

Beyond the trade routes lies an entirely different world.

There, currents become unpredictable, landmarks disappear, wildlife grows stranger and stories become increasingly difficult to separate from truth.

This is where explorers, pirates, treasure hunters and adventurers earn their reputations.

---

# The People of Aetheris

Most people never leave the island on which they were born.

Travel is expensive.

Ships are valuable.

The dangers of the Voidsea are well understood.

For many, there is simply no reason to leave behind family, work and community.

Those who choose a life at sea accept risks that most people would never willingly face.

---

# Ships

Owning a skyship is beyond the reach of most individuals.

Merchant guilds commission vessels for trade.

Military powers maintain fleets.

Pirates steal what they cannot build.

Scavengers and independent captains often spend decades saving for ageing vessels barely capable of making the safest routes.

Ships are frequently passed from one generation to the next, becoming part of a family's history as much as its livelihood.

---

# Learning the Sea

Most children receive a basic education about the major trade routes and the importance of the currents.

Those who pursue a life at sea continue their education through apprenticeships, guilds, naval service or family tradition.

No amount of study can replace experience.

The greatest navigators are respected not because they know every current, but because they recognise when the Voidsea is beginning to change.

---

# Culture

The Voidsea is feared.

It is respected.

It is endlessly discussed.

Stories, rumours and superstitions pass from crew to crew until nobody remembers where they began.

Many cultures warn children never to stare too long over the edge of an island.

Others believe the Void listens.

Some claim that rainwater flowing from rivers and waterfalls eventually disappears into the darkness below, though nobody knows where it ultimately goes.

Few questions inspire more debate than what truly lies beneath the world.

---

# Sailors

Sailors occupy a unique place within Aetherian society.

They are respected for keeping civilisation connected while quietly accepting dangers that most islanders never face.

Children dream of becoming famous captains or infamous pirates.

Parents are often proud of such ambitions, but that pride is tempered by fear.

Every family knows of someone who never returned from the Voidsea.

---

# Sailor's Wisdom

Among experienced crews there is one saying that needs no explanation.

> "The Voidsea is quiet."

No sailor wishes to be the first to speak those words.

For when the Voidsea falls silent, even the most experienced captains begin looking toward the horizon.

---

## What this means for Session 1

Players should naturally discover these assumptions through conversation rather than exposition.

Dockworkers casually refer to named currents.

Merchants discuss shipping schedules as ordinary business.

Families worry when loved ones depart on voyages.

Children watch famous captains with admiration.

Veteran sailors become noticeably uneasy when someone remarks that the Voidsea feels unusually quiet.

The world should feel lived in long before the players decide to leave the safety of the trade routes.

## Questions this raises

- How are currents discovered?

- Why do some currents change?
