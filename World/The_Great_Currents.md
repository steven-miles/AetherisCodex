# The Great Currents

## Purpose

This document describes the major currents that converge on Port Meridian during Sprint 1.

These are not the only currents in Aetheris. They are simply the routes that every sailor, merchant and navigator is likely to know, and therefore the ones most likely to be encountered or referenced during Session 1.

Future sprints will expand the wider network of currents across Aetheris.

---

# All Skies Lead to Meridian

There is an old saying known across Aetheris:

> "All skies lead to Meridian."

It is not literally true.

Rather, it reflects the fact that Port Meridian sits at the greatest confluence of known currents. For centuries this made it the natural meeting place of merchants, explorers, scholars and travellers from every corner of the known world.

Meridian was not built because it was important.

It became important because the currents already were.

---

# Currents and Culture

Currents are more than routes.

They are cultural highways.

People living along the same current trade frequently, share stories, influence one another's customs and often develop similar expressions, fashions and traditions.

The further two islands are separated by the network of currents, the more different they tend to become.

---

# The Lantern Flow

The safest and most travelled current leaving Port Meridian.

Every apprentice navigator learns to follow the Lantern Flow before attempting more demanding routes.

Its reliability has made it the backbone of trade for countless generations.

Reputation:
- Reliable
- Busy
- Welcoming
- Ideal for new crews

---

# The King's Wake

A long, graceful current favoured by wealthy merchants and experienced captains who value consistency over speed.

The voyage is renowned for spectacular dawns and sunsets.

Reputation:
- Elegant
- Predictable
- Prestigious
- Comfortable

---

# Widow's Crossing

The fastest of Meridian's major routes.

Its speed comes at a cost.

Crossing requires precise navigation and calm judgement, particularly where several currents intersect.

Every sailor knows someone who failed to complete Widow's Crossing.

Reputation:
- Fast
- Dangerous
- Respected
- Unforgiving

---

# Pilgrim's Reach

A gentle route linking numerous smaller islands and communities.

Scholars, pilgrims, missionaries and travelling performers commonly favour this current over the faster trade routes.

Many sailors claim that no other current better represents everyday life in Aetheris.

Reputation:
- Peaceful
- Welcoming
- Community focused
- Scenic

---

# The Broken Song

Perfectly charted.

Perfectly safe.

Almost universally disliked.

Crews speak more quietly while travelling this current, though few can explain why.

Some swear they hear distant music carried through the Void.

Others insist the silence itself is unsettling.

The Navigation Guild dismisses such stories as superstition.

Reputation:
- Quiet
- Unsettling
- Mysterious
- Avoided when alternatives exist

# The Language of Currents

Experienced sailors rarely think in compass directions.

Instead, they think in currents.

A navigator is far more likely to say:

> "Catch the Lantern Flow."

than

> "Head east."

The currents shape how people understand the world itself. Islands are remembered by the routes that reach them rather than by their position on a map.

Over generations, this has influenced language and identity.

People may describe themselves by the waters they know best.

Examples include:

- "She's a Lantern sailor."
- "He learned his trade on Widow's Crossing."
- "Anyone who's mastered the King's Wake can handle any merchant route."

To experienced crews, these phrases immediately communicate a navigator's background, experience and reputation.

---

# Beyond the Great Currents

The Great Currents are only the best known routes in Aetheris.

Countless regional currents connect nearby islands.

Some are known only to local fishing communities.

Others exist only on Captain's Charts.

Still others are whispered about in rumour, appearing on no official chart at all.

As sailors move farther from the great trade routes, navigation becomes less about following established paths and more about knowledge, instinct and courage.

# Questions this raises

- What lies beyond each of these routes?
- Why does the Broken Song make sailors uneasy?
- Which current is the oldest?
- Are there forgotten Great Currents?
- Which current first led sailors to Port Meridian?

## What this means for Session 1

Dockworkers, merchants and sailors should casually refer to these currents by name.

Characters should hear sayings such as "Catch the Lantern Flow" instead of compass directions.

Players should quickly realise that navigators think in currents rather than geography, and that every current carries its own stories, reputation and culture.
