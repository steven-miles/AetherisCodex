# Navigation and Currents

## Purpose

This document explains how the people of Aetheris think about navigation. It focuses on culture and shared understanding rather than mechanical rules.

---

# The Currents

Invisible currents flow throughout the Voidsea.

They are natural phenomena that have existed for as long as history records. Scholars have proposed countless theories for their existence, but none have been proven.

To the people of Aetheris, the currents simply *are*. Like rivers, they are accepted as a fundamental part of the world.

---

# Following the Currents

Skyships travel by catching these currents with their magical engines.

A skilled navigator is responsible for reading the currents and choosing the safest route.

Travelling with an inexperienced navigator is possible, but often slower, less efficient, and considerably more dangerous.

A skilled navigator is often worth more to a voyage than the finest ship.

---

# The Trade Routes

The great trade routes are formed by the safest and most reliable currents.

These routes are stable enough for commerce to flourish, though experienced navigators know they may shift subtly over time.

Leaving the trade routes means leaving the known world.

---

# The Navigation Guild

The Navigation Guild records, verifies and publishes official navigation charts used throughout Aetheris.

These charts are shared publicly because civilisation depends upon safe and reliable trade.

The Guild's charts represent the accumulated knowledge of generations of navigators.

---

# Types of Charts

## Guild Charts

Official charts published by the Navigation Guild.

Reliable, trusted and widely available.

## Captain's Charts

Personal charts maintained by individual captains.

These contain handwritten observations, shortcuts, seasonal notes, wildlife migrations, dangerous crossings and other hard-earned knowledge.

Many are passed through families or sold for considerable sums.

## Forbidden Charts

Rumoured to exist.

These supposedly record forgotten routes, dangerous currents, hidden islands and places that the Guild refuses to recognise or publish.

Whether these charts truly exist remains a matter of debate.

---

# Crossing Currents

Currents frequently intersect.

These junctions require careful coordination between ships and are among the busiest places on the Voidsea.

Experienced navigators prepare well in advance to safely transfer between currents and avoid collisions.

---

# Anchoring

Skyships anchor using magical anchors that bind to a fixed point in space rather than the ground below.

This allows vessels to safely stop even while suspended above the Void.

---

# Naming Currents

Currents are traditionally named after the stories surrounding their discovery rather than geographical features.

Many names have outlived the tales that inspired them.

Over time each current develops its own reputation among sailors.

Some are considered gentle.

Others unforgiving.

Others simply... unsettling.

---

# Reputation

Experienced navigators rarely speak in terms of directions.

Instead they speak in currents.

"Catch the Lantern Flow."

"Leave the King's Wake before dusk."

"The Broken Song is quiet this season."

A navigator's experience is often judged by the famous currents they have sailed rather than the number of miles they have travelled.

---

# Questions this raises

- What caused the currents to form?
- Why do some currents subtly shift over time?
- What happened that convinced the Navigation Guild to publish its charts?
- Do Forbidden Charts truly exist?
- Which famous currents have the greatest reputations?
- How does someone become a Master Navigator?

## What this means for Session 1

Players should hear sailors referring to currents by name instead of compass directions.

Navigation should feel like a respected profession.

The Navigation Guild should be spoken of with trust and respect.

The existence of Captain's Charts should hint that valuable knowledge is a commodity in Aetheris.
