# Session 1 - Mechanics

> Narrative direction belongs in `SESSION_FLOW.md`.

---

## Beat 1 — Awakening

No required mechanics.

Avoid checks for basic observation.

Let players:

- ask questions
- inspect their surroundings
- discover their Voidmarks
- interact with other arrivals
- prepare themselves

Do not require rolls unless failure would create an interesting consequence.

---

## Beat 2 — Raid on the Current

Run:

`Encounters/ENCOUNTER 001 - Raid on the Current`

This is the first guaranteed combat opportunity of the Chronicle.

Encounter balance is determined by the party-specific variant.

Before running the encounter, use the appropriate file in:

`Encounters/ENCOUNTER 001 - Raid on the Current/Variants/`

The purpose is to:

- let players test their characters
- introduce combat in Aetheris
- reveal the Void
- establish morale and retreat
- allow player creativity


### Player Participation

The party is never forced to fight.

They may:

- help the guards
- defend civilians
- board the scavenger vessel
- negotiate
- intimidate
- refuse to participate
- attempt another solution

The encounter continues naturally from their choice.

### Environmental Checks

Use the encounter's map guidance.

General DC reference:

- DC 10 — straightforward under pressure
- DC 12 — requires some skill
- DC 15 — difficult or risky
- DC 18 — exceptional

Do not call for a roll when failure would not matter.

Do not raise a DC simply because a creative action would be powerful.

### Morale

The scavengers do not fight to the death.

They retreat when the raid has clearly failed.

Possible retreat triggers include:

- several scavengers being defeated
- the leader being badly injured
- the scavenger vessel becoming threatened
- the boarding route being compromised
- successful intimidation
- overwhelming player power

### Falling Into The Void

Falling should feel extremely dangerous.

Do not treat a single failed movement check as automatic death.

Allow reasonable reactions where appropriate:

- grabbing a railing
- catching a rope
- another character intervening
- magic
- another creative solution

Do not invent new Void lore during the encounter.

### Encounter Outcome

After combat, record:

- whether the party participated
- whether scavengers escaped or surrendered
- whether anyone was killed
- whether civilians were protected
- whether excessive violence occurred
- whether the scavenger vessel was captured
- what Captain Vex Sterling reports

Use the encounter's `CONSEQUENCES.md`.

## Beat 3 — First Steps

Registration is primarily roleplay.

No required checks.

Thomas first meets the party on the docks of Port Meridian.

He is the Dockmaster.

His first impression may be influenced by Captain Vex Sterling's report.

### Registration

Clerks ask for:

- name
- place of origin

Unknown locations are recorded as:

**Unknown**

No check is required.

### Edith

Interaction with Edith is roleplay unless the party attempts something outside normal procedure.

### Captain Vex's Report

The report moves through Port Meridian's normal administrative chain.

What reaches Thomas and later the Governor depends on what actually happened during the raid.

Do not predetermine the party's reputation.

---

## Beat 4 — The Governor

No persuasion check is required for information the Governor already intends to share.

Checks may be appropriate if players try to:

- negotiate resources
- pry into information he is deliberately withholding
- pressure policy changes
- obtain additional official support

The raid is not the reason the missing island exists.

It simply gives the Governor reason to believe the party may be capable.

### Governor's Token

The party receives a simple wooden token bearing the Governor's mark.

It may:

- establish that the party has his permission
- open conversations or restricted areas
- improve first reactions from officials
- remove unnecessary bureaucracy

It does not automatically win social encounters.

## Beat 6 — Finding a Way Forward

The party is trying to determine how to reach the missing island.

Do not tell them they need their own ship.

Let them arrive at that conclusion naturally.

### Possible Checks

Depending on approach:

- Persuasion
- Insight
- Investigation
- Deception
- Intimidation

Do not require all of them.

Let approach determine mechanics.

### Joshua

Joshua can naturally point the party towards the Driftwrights if they ask about old, cheap or unwanted ships.

No check is required if the party has reached a point where Joshua would reasonably help.

## Beat 7 — Claiming the Sea Bird

Run:

`Encounters/ENCOUNTER 002 - Claiming the Ship`

The Sea Bird is owned by the Driftwrights.

Arthur ultimately decides whether they are willing to part with it.

Possible solutions include:

- buy it
- work for it
- negotiate
- persuade
- promise future work
- appeal to Arthur's values
- earn respect
- intimidate
- steal
- fight
- invent another solution

Do not punish clever non-combat solutions.

### Social Checks

Do not turn Arthur into a single Persuasion DC.

Roleplay first.

Checks should support the players' approach rather than replace it.

A strong argument may:

- lower a DC
- remove the need for a roll
- change Arthur's position outright

Likewise, poor behaviour may make agreement more difficult.

### Optional Combat

Combat is possible but not required.

If combat begins:

- Driftwrights value life
- surrender is valid
- non-lethal combat is valid
- damaging ships matters
- attacking surrendered people has consequences

Use Encounter 002's creature and tactics files.

### Ship Condition

The Sea Bird is:

- an old merchant vessel
- currently undergoing repairs
- structurally sound enough to restore
- capable of becoming the party's long-term home

It can eventually support:

- a crew of around twenty
- weapons
- cargo
- upgraded systems
- player-designed improvements

Do not fully define future ship mechanics during Session 1.

## Beat 8 — First Departure

A small temporary crew helps the party leave Meridian safely.

Possible roles:

- steering
- navigation
- engine
- lines
- lookout
- deck work

Every player should have something meaningful to do.

Formal ship mechanics can be developed later.

### Launch Bell

The player chosen to act as Captain rings the bell before departure.

No check required.

This is ceremony, not mechanics.

### Rookie Captain Mistake

If appropriate, the inexperienced Captain may forget to slow before engaging the magical anchor.

This can result in:

**SCREECH.**

A violent but non-dangerous stop.

Use as humour if it feels natural.

Do not force it if the players have already established competence.

### Catching The Current

The first entry into an Arcane Current is primarily narrative.

No formal navigation mechanics are required during Session 1.

Use the scene to establish:

- how the Current feels
- how the ship responds
- how much coordination a crew requires

### Milestone

Recommended milestone:

The party acquires the Sea Bird and successfully departs Port Meridian.

### Session End

A distant island is visible ahead.

The party does not reach it during Session 1.
