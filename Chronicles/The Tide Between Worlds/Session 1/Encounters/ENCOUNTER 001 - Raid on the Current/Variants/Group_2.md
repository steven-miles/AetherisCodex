# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Group 2 Variant

> Same raid.
>
> Smaller fight.

---

# Party

**Party Size:** 3

**Character Level:** 3

**Target Difficulty:** Easy

---

# Purpose

This configuration preserves the narrative scale of the Raid on the Current while reducing the mechanical threat for a smaller, lower-level party.

The players should still experience:

- A vessel under attack.
- Multiple scavengers.
- The first reveal of the Void.
- Ship-to-ship combat.
- Enemy morale.
- Retreat.
- Opportunities for creative movement and environmental actions.

The raid should feel larger than the number of creatures actually fighting the player characters.

---

# Important Principle

There may be many scavengers involved in the raid.

Only a few are actively fighting the party.

Other scavengers remain occupied with:

- The passenger guards.
- Grappling lines.
- The gangplank.
- Stolen cargo.
- Their own vessel.
- Preparing their escape.

This allows the encounter to look like a genuine boarding action without placing an overwhelming number of enemies into initiative.

---

# Encounter Composition

Begin with:

- 1 Group 2 Scavenger Boss
- 2 Scavenger Raiders

**3 active hostile creatures**

Use the normal Scavenger Raider statistics from:

`../CREATURES.md`

Use the modified Boss below.

---

# Starting Positions

## Passenger Vessel

Begin with:

- 2 Scavenger Raiders

One should be relatively close to the boarding route.

The other may be threatening a guard or searching for valuables.

---

## Scavenger Vessel

Begin with:

- Group 2 Scavenger Boss

The Boss initially remains aboard the scavenger vessel.

---

# Visible Scavengers

Place or describe approximately:

**2 additional Scavenger Raiders**

aboard the scavenger vessel.

These are not initially active combatants.

They are:

- Managing grappling lines.
- Helping control the vessel.
- Watching the fight.
- Preparing the escape.

Their presence makes the raid feel larger.

Do not roll initiative for them unless they actually join the fight.

---

# Group 2 Scavenger Boss

*Medium Humanoid*

The Boss organised the raid.

They expected frightened passengers.

Three capable adventurers were already more resistance than they planned for.

---

**Armor Class:** 13

**Hit Points:** 32

**Speed:** 30 ft.

| 14 (+2) | 14 (+2) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) | 12 (+1) | 14 (+2) |

**Passive Perception:** 13

## Actions

### Cutlass

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 6 (1d8 + 2) slashing damage.

The Group 2 Boss does **not** have Multiattack.

---

### Move!

**Recharge 5–6**

The Boss chooses one scavenger within 30 feet who can hear them.

That scavenger may immediately move up to half their speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

This should primarily be used to:

- Pull an injured Raider away.
- Protect an escape route.
- Begin retreating.

It should not be used to aggressively overwhelm a player character.

---

# Boss Behaviour

The Boss initially believes the two Raiders can handle the situation.

They may:

- Shout instructions.
- Demand valuables.
- Threaten the passengers.
- Remain close to the gangplank.

The Boss enters direct combat only once it becomes clear that the Raiders need help or the party threatens the scavenger vessel.

The Boss should become concerned quickly.

This was supposed to be easy.

---

# Reinforcements

Do not automatically add reinforcements.

The visible scavengers aboard the second vessel exist as a difficulty control.

---

## If The Party Is Dominating

If:

- Nobody has taken meaningful damage.
- A Raider is defeated immediately.
- The players are clearly comfortable.
- Combat would benefit from another threat.

Then:

**1 additional Scavenger Raider may join the battle.**

Describe them seeing the fight deteriorate and rushing to help.

Do not add both reserve Raiders at once.

---

## If The Fight Is Balanced

Do nothing.

The remaining scavengers stay aboard their vessel.

---

## If The Party Is Struggling

Do not reinforce.

Instead:

- The Boss becomes nervous.
- Scavengers begin withdrawing.
- Passenger guards create distractions.
- The retreat may occur early.

The encounter should not become a fight to rescue the party from an accidental difficulty spike.

---

# Passenger Guards

The guards remain mostly cinematic.

For Group 2, they are also a useful safety valve.

If the party is struggling badly, a guard may:

- Distract a Raider.
- Block an attack route.
- Help an injured player reach safety.
- Keep a reserve scavenger occupied.

Do not have guards suddenly defeat the encounter.

Their role is to make the surrounding battle believable and prevent an unlucky round from becoming catastrophic.

---

# Morale

Group 2's scavengers should break earlier than Group 1's.

Begin considering retreat when:

- 1 Raider is defeated and another is badly injured.
- Both starting Raiders are defeated.
- The Boss falls below half hit points.
- The Boss is subjected to a frightening or powerful ability.
- A player boards the scavenger vessel.
- The gangplank or grappling lines are threatened.
- The party successfully intimidates them.
- Continuing clearly no longer makes sense.

Do not wait for a fixed hit-point threshold if the raid has obviously failed.

---

# Retreat

Once retreat begins:

The remaining scavengers prioritise escape immediately.

They:

- Disengage.
- Pull injured allies away.
- Cross the gangplank.
- Use grappling ropes.
- Cut lines.
- Abandon stolen valuables.

The reserve scavengers aboard the vessel begin preparing to leave rather than joining combat.

---

# If A Player Falls

The scavengers do not attack unconscious characters.

If one of three player characters falls unconscious, the encounter has changed significantly.

Immediately reassess.

Unless the remaining players are comfortably winning:

Do not add reinforcements.

Consider beginning the scavengers' retreat earlier.

---

# If Two Players Fall

The scavengers no longer need to continue fighting.

They believe they have won.

They may:

- Grab readily available valuables.
- Withdraw.
- Leave the remaining conscious character alone if they stop resisting.

They do not execute anyone.

The encounter can end in defeat without ending the Chronicle.

---

# Environmental Actions

Use the same map and environmental DC guidance as the shared encounter.

For Group 2, be particularly generous with creative solutions.

Examples include:

- Cutting a grappling line.
- Removing the gangplank.
- Swinging between vessels.
- Dropping cargo into an enemy's path.
- Intimidating the scavengers.
- Using magic to separate the ships.

A clever action that removes an enemy from the encounter is a success.

Do not replace that enemy merely because the planned encounter contained three opponents.

---

# Encounter Length

Aim for approximately:

**2–4 meaningful combat rounds**

This is not a strict requirement.

If the encounter ends in one spectacular round:

Let it.

If roleplay and environmental actions stretch the scene longer:

Let that happen too.

Do not keep feeding enemies into combat to reach an arbitrary length.

---

# Expected Experience

The party should feel:

**Outnumbered by the raid**

without being:

**Outmatched by the encounter.**

The surrounding ship battle creates scale.

The three active opponents create manageable mechanics.

The players should leave the fight feeling capable rather than lucky to be alive.
