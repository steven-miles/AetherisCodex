# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Loot

> "One person's junk is another world's treasure."

---

# Purpose

The scavengers did not attack the passenger vessel because they were wealthy.

They attacked because of rumours.

People have been arriving in Aetheris from other worlds.

Some arrive carrying objects, materials and possessions unlike anything found locally.

The scavengers believe those objects could be valuable.

What the party finds aboard the scavenger vessel should reinforce this.

---

# Loot Philosophy

This encounter should not provide a large monetary reward.

The scavengers are desperate.

If they were wealthy, they would not be risking their lives attacking a passenger vessel on a major Current near Port Meridian.

Their possessions are mostly:

- Practical equipment.
- Previously scavenged goods.
- Small amounts of coin.
- Strange objects they believe may have value.
- Things nobody else particularly wanted.

The interesting part of the loot is what it tells the players about Aetheris.

---

# On The Scavengers

Individual scavengers carry very little.

Between the entire boarding party, the players may find:

- A small amount of mixed Aetherian coin.
- Worn knives.
- Basic tools.
- Rope.
- Food.
- Personal trinkets.
- Empty sacks intended for stolen goods.

Do not treat every scavenger as an individual loot container.

If the players search everyone, summarise what they find.

---

# The Scavenger Vessel

If the players board or capture the scavenger vessel, they find a collection of salvaged goods.

Most have little immediate value.

Possible contents include:

- Spare rope.
- Damaged tools.
- Scrap metal.
- Timber offcuts.
- Engine components.
- Empty barrels.
- Old clothing.
- Cracked containers.
- Repair materials.
- A small amount of preserved food.
- Several sacks prepared for the raid.

Nothing suggests wealth.

The vessel looks like something maintained by people who cannot afford to replace anything until it completely stops working.

---

# Objects From Other Worlds

Among the scavenged goods are several objects clearly unlike ordinary Aetherian possessions.

They were taken from previous arrivals or traded through the underworld.

Examples may include:

- An unfamiliar coin.
- A broken mechanical object.
- A fragment of unusual material.
- A piece of jewellery in an unfamiliar style.
- A small religious symbol.
- An object whose purpose nobody aboard understands.

These items do not need immediate explanations.

Their presence establishes something important:

**Things brought through from other worlds circulate through Aetheris.**

Some people collect them.

Some study them.

Some sell them.

Some simply think they might be worth something.

---

# Player Possessions

The scavengers are particularly interested in anything the player characters possess that appears unusual.

This may include:

- Weapons.
- Jewellery.
- Currency.
- Religious objects.
- Tools.
- Clothing.
- Books.
- Magical objects.
- Mundane objects unfamiliar to Aetheris.

The scavengers do not necessarily understand what these things are.

They only understand that unusual things can sometimes be sold.

---

# If Questioned

A captured scavenger can explain:

> "People come through carrying things."

A shrug.

> "Things we've never seen."

> "Sometimes they're worth money."

If asked where they heard about the new arrivals:

> "People talk."

They do not possess secret information.

There is no conspiracy behind the raid.

The scavengers heard rumours and made a desperate decision.

---

# Coin

The scavengers possess only a modest amount of money.

Do not assign an exact value unless the players specifically search for it.

If necessary, provide enough to feel believable but not enough to materially change the party's finances.

The scavengers are poor.

That should remain obvious.

---

# Weapons

The scavengers' weapons are functional but poorly maintained.

They may be taken and used normally.

They have no special properties.

Merchants in Port Meridian are unlikely to pay much for them.

---

# The Passenger Vessel

The party is not automatically entitled to anything aboard the passenger vessel.

The ship belongs to Captain Vex Sterling and its owners.

Its cargo belongs to passengers, merchants and other legitimate owners.

Taking items from the passenger vessel after defending it is still theft.

The world should respond accordingly.

---

# Rewards From Captain Vex Sterling

Captain Vex does not provide a large monetary reward.

The party defended the vessel because they chose to help.

Vex's immediate priorities are:

- Injured passengers.
- Ship repairs.
- Reaching Port Meridian safely.
- Reporting the attack.

The party receives something more useful than money:

**Vex's account of what they did.**

That report helps establish their reputation upon arrival.

---

# Capturing The Scavenger Vessel

It is possible that the players completely derail the expected encounter and capture the scavenger vessel.

Allow this.

Do not prevent it simply because the Chronicle did not expect it.

However:

The vessel is:

- Poorly maintained.
- Damaged.
- Unreliable.
- Recognisable.
- Connected to a crime committed near Port Meridian.

Possessing it creates questions rather than providing a free replacement for the Sea Bird.

The authorities will want to know what happened.

The vessel may become:

- Evidence.
- Confiscated property.
- Salvage.
- A bargaining opportunity.
- Something else determined by player actions and Aetherian law.

Resolve this naturally during play.

---

# Unexpected Loot

If the players become fascinated by an ordinary object aboard the scavenger vessel:

Let them take it.

Not every interesting possession needs hidden significance.

Sometimes an old compass is simply an old compass.

If the players make it important through their actions...

It can become important later.

---

# DM Reminder

The greatest reward from this encounter is not money.

The players gain:

- Their first victory in Aetheris.
- Their first reputation.
- Their first glimpse of the Void.
- Evidence that objects from their home worlds have value.
- A reason for Port Meridian's authorities to notice them.

The loot supports the world.

It does not define the encounter.
