# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Creatures

> The scavengers expected frightened passengers and easy valuables.
>
> They have seriously misjudged the situation.

---

# Purpose

This file contains the shared creature templates used in **Raid on the Current**.

It does not define:

- Party size.
- Character level.
- Encounter difficulty.
- Enemy numbers.
- Starting positions.
- Reinforcement limits.
- Party-specific stat changes.

Those belong in the appropriate encounter variant.

Use:

- `Variants/GROUP_1.md`
- `Variants/GROUP_2.md`

The scavengers' personalities, motivations and general combat behaviour remain consistent between groups.

---

# Scavenger Raider

*Medium Humanoid*

Scavenger Raiders make up most of the boarding party.

They rely on numbers, intimidation and aggression rather than exceptional combat ability.

---

**Armor Class:** 13

**Hit Points:** 22

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 (+2) | 14 (+2) | 12 (+1) | 10 (+0) | 11 (+0) | 10 (+0) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +4

**Passive Perception:** 10

---

## Actions

### Rusted Blade

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 6 (1d8 + 2) slashing damage.

---

### Shove

The Raider attempts to shove a creature within 5 feet.

**Athletics:** +4

Use normal contested shove rules.

Raiders prefer knocking opponents away from valuable cargo or boarding routes rather than pushing people into the Void.

---

## Behaviour

Raiders are confident while they believe they have control.

Once seriously injured or clearly outmatched, they attempt to retreat.

They do not attack unconscious creatures.

They are thieves and scavengers, not executioners.

# Scavenger Scrapper

*Medium Humanoid*

Scrappers are larger scavengers used to forcing open cargo holds, moving heavy goods and intimidating frightened crews.

---

**Armor Class:** 13

**Hit Points:** 35

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 (+3) | 11 (+0) | 14 (+2) | 9 (-1) | 11 (+0) | 10 (+0) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +5

**Passive Perception:** 10

---

## Actions

### Heavy Club

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 8 (1d10 + 3) bludgeoning damage.

---

### Get Back!

Instead of attacking, the Scrapper may attempt to shove a creature within 5 feet.

**Athletics:** +5

If successful, the Scrapper may immediately move up to 10 feet without provoking an opportunity attack from that creature.

This is primarily used when attempting to retreat.

---

## Behaviour

Scrappers place themselves between weaker scavengers and dangerous opponents.

They are intimidating rather than bloodthirsty.

When retreat is called, they attempt to ensure others escape before following.

They do not attack unconscious creatures.

# Scavenger Boss

*Medium Humanoid*

The Boss organised the raid.

They expected an easy score.

They are rapidly beginning to regret that decision.

This is the standard Scavenger Boss template.

Party-specific variants may replace or modify this stat block where required.

---

**Armor Class:** 14

**Hit Points:** 52

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 (+2) | 16 (+3) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) | 12 (+1) | 14 (+2) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +4, Intimidation +4, Perception +3

**Passive Perception:** 13

---

## Actions

### Multiattack

The Boss makes two Cutlass attacks.

### Cutlass

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 7 (1d8 + 3) slashing damage.

---

### Move!

**Recharge 5-6**

The Boss chooses one scavenger within 30 feet who can hear them.

That scavenger may immediately move up to half its speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

---

## Reaction

### Not Worth Dying For

When a scavenger within 10 feet of the Boss is reduced to 0 hit points, the Boss may immediately move up to half their speed towards the scavenger vessel without provoking opportunity attacks.

The Boss is not heroic.

They know when things are going badly.

---

## Behaviour

The Boss begins confident.

Their priorities are:

1. Control the raid.
2. Secure valuables.
3. Prevent the boarding party from panicking.
4. Escape when the raid clearly fails.

The Boss should never behave like a villain making a final stand.

If survival becomes more important than loot, they abandon the raid.

---

# Shared Scavenger Morale

The scavengers should not fight to the death.

Exact retreat thresholds may differ between party variants.

Regardless of group, retreat becomes appropriate when:

- The raid has obviously failed.
- The Boss is badly injured or defeated.
- The scavenger vessel becomes threatened.
- Their escape route becomes endangered.
- The party displays overwhelming power.
- The players successfully intimidate them.
- Continuing the fight no longer makes sense.

The DM does not need to wait for a specific mechanical trigger.

If the raid has obviously failed:

**Call the retreat.**

Party-specific guidance belongs in the appropriate variant file.

# Surrender

A scavenger who cannot reasonably escape may surrender.

They:

- Drop their weapon.
- Raise their hands.
- Ask not to be killed.

They will readily explain why they attacked.

They heard rumours that people from other worlds were arriving with unusual possessions.

They thought the passengers would be easy targets.

They were wrong.

Surrender is genuine unless events at the table give the DM a reason to establish otherwise.

---

# Unconscious Characters

The scavengers do not deliberately attack unconscious player characters.

Their priorities are:

- Escape.
- Loot.
- Survival.
- Protecting their own people.

They have no reason to execute fallen opponents.

Party-specific variants may provide additional safety guidance for smaller groups.

# Passenger Ship Guards

The guards exist primarily in the background.

If statistics are required:

**Armor Class:** 15

**Hit Points:** 18

**Attack:** +4 to hit

**Damage:** 5 (1d6 + 2)

Do not run every guard as a full combatant unless necessary.

Describe their fighting elsewhere on the deck.

The player characters should remain the focus.

The guards can also explain why additional scavengers are present without requiring every enemy on the map to enter initiative.

---

# Captain Vex Sterling

Captain Vex Sterling commands the passenger vessel.

Vex is an experienced working captain.

No unusual combat ability is required.

During the raid, Vex's priorities are:

1. Keep the vessel safely within the Current.
2. Protect the passengers.
3. Coordinate the crew.
4. Prevent catastrophic damage.

Vex does not join the player characters unless absolutely necessary.

If statistics unexpectedly become necessary, use the Passenger Ship Guard statistics.

# Environmental Actions

Do not create a fixed list of special environmental actions.

Players may attempt anything reasonable involving:

- Grappling ropes.
- Gangplank.
- Rigging.
- Railings.
- Cargo.
- The other vessel.
- The Void.

Set an appropriate DC based on the difficulty of the attempted action.

As a general guideline:

**DC 10** — straightforward under pressure

**DC 12** — requires some skill

**DC 15** — difficult or risky

**DC 18** — exceptional

Do not increase a DC simply because an idea would significantly help the players.

Creative solutions are part of the encounter.

If a clever environmental action effectively removes an enemy from the fight, allow that success to matter.

Do not automatically replace them with another enemy.

---

# Falling Into The Void

Falling from either vessel should feel terrifying.

It should not automatically mean death.

If a character falls:

Give nearby characters an immediate opportunity to react where reasonable.

Possible solutions include:

- Grab them.
- Throw a rope.
- Use magic.
- Swing after them.
- Catch them with rigging.
- Attempt another reasonable solution.

The exact mechanics of prolonged exposure or falling through the Void should follow established Aetheris rules.

Do not invent new Void lore during this encounter.

---

# Running The Creatures

Keep turns fast.

Scavenger Raiders should usually:

**Move → Attack → React to what just happened**

Scrappers should:

**Protect → Shove → Create space**

The Boss should:

**Command → Fight → Realise this was a terrible idea → Retreat**

The complexity should come from the players.

Not the enemies.
