# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Encounter Flow

> This document combines narration, encounter beats and director notes.
>
> It is not a rigid script.
>
> Let the players determine how the battle unfolds.

---

# Beat 1 - Something Is Wrong

## Purpose

Interrupt the relative safety of the awakening.

The players should immediately understand that something unexpected has happened.

---

## Scene

The party is still below deck.

They have only recently awakened.

Thomas and the crew are helping confused passengers.

Some people are asking questions.

Others are still trying to understand where they are.

Then—

**BANG.**

Something strikes the ship.

---

## Suggested Narration

> "The ship suddenly lurches beneath you."

[Pause]

> "Something heavy strikes the hull."

> "Hard."

A few passengers stumble.

Someone screams.

Above them:

> "Boots."

> "Running."

Then shouting.

Another impact.

**BANG.**

This time closer.

The timber groans.

---

## Thomas

Thomas immediately becomes focused.

He does not panic.

He begins moving civilians further into the hold.

> "Everyone stay calm."

To someone near the stairs:

> "Keep them away from the hatch."

He does not yet know exactly what is happening.

---

# Beat 2 - The Call For Help

## Purpose

Give the players agency.

Nobody assumes they are adventurers.

Nobody orders them to fight.

They choose.

---

## Scene

The hatch opens.

A member of the ship's guard appears.

They are breathing heavily.

Behind them comes the sound of shouting.

> "Thomas!"

> "We've got boarders!"

They look around the hold.

Then:

> "We need help up here!"

Thomas looks towards the newly arrived passengers.

Then towards the party.

He does not know what they are capable of.

---

## Thomas

> "If you think you can help..."

[Pause]

> "...please do."

He points further into the hold.

> "If not, get below deck."

Then he immediately returns to moving civilians.

---

## Director's Notes

Do not force the players upstairs.

They may:

- immediately help
- prepare first
- ask questions
- protect civilians
- remain below
- attempt something unexpected

The battle exists regardless.

The players decide how they respond.

---

# Beat 3 - First Sight Of The Void

## Purpose

Reveal Aetheris during action rather than exposition.

This should be the party's first true understanding that they are no longer in their own world.

---

## Suggested Narration

When the first player reaches the deck:

> "You climb through the hatch..."

> "...and the noise hits you."

[Pause]

> "Shouting."

> "Boots hammering against timber."

> "Steel leaving scabbards."

> "Ropes pulled tight."

A second vessel hangs alongside the passenger ship.

> "Thick ropes stretch between the two ships."

> "Heavy hooks have buried themselves into the railings."

A crude gangplank connects the vessels.

Several figures are already crossing.

Then allow the players to notice what surrounds the ships.

> "And beyond them..."

[Pause]

> "There is no ocean."

Long pause.

> "There is no water at all."

> "Beneath the ships..."

> "...an endless black Void stretches away beneath the world."

Before they can fully process it:

> "A figure grabs one of the ropes connecting the vessels."

> "They leap."

> "Swinging across the impossible drop..."

> "...and land hard on the deck."

They look towards the party.

Their expression changes.

They clearly weren't expecting resistance.

---

# Beat 4 - The Raid

## Purpose

Begin combat.

Give the players room to experiment with their Level 5 characters.

---

## Starting Situation

The scavengers have already begun boarding.

The passenger ship's guards are trying to hold them back.

The crew are protecting passengers and keeping control of the vessel.

Thomas remains primarily concerned with civilians.

---

## Battlefield

The players have access to:

- Both ships.
- The gangplank.
- Grappling ropes.
- Railings.
- Rigging.
- Cargo crates.
- Deck structures.
- Ship equipment.

Do not tell players what they can do with these things.

Describe the environment.

Let them invent solutions.

---

## Crossing Between Ships

Characters may attempt to cross using:

- The gangplank.
- Grappling ropes.
- Rigging.
- Magic.
- Flight.
- Teleportation.
- Any reasonable method they invent.

Resolve unusual actions using normal checks where appropriate.

The Void below should feel dangerous.

Do not make creativity unnecessarily difficult.

---

# Beat 5 - The Scavengers Realise Their Mistake

## Purpose

Change the emotional direction of the battle.

The scavengers entered expecting frightened civilians.

They have encountered six experienced combatants.

---

## Director's Notes

The scavengers should initially appear confident.

As powerful abilities begin appearing, their behaviour changes.

A scavenger might react to an impressive spell with:

> "What the hells was that?!"

Another:

> "You said they were passengers!"

Another:

> "THEY ARE PASSENGERS!"

Allow some humour here.

The scavengers have made a terrible mistake.

---

## Morale

As the battle turns against them:

- wounded scavengers move towards escape routes
- scavengers begin helping injured allies
- confidence disappears
- attacks become defensive
- protecting loot stops mattering

They are no longer trying to win.

They are trying to leave.

---

# Beat 6 - Retreat

## Purpose

Teach the players that victory does not require extermination.

---

## Trigger

When the scavengers clearly understand that the raid has failed, their leader orders the retreat.

The exact trigger should remain flexible.

Possible triggers include:

- roughly half the boarding force being defeated
- the leader being badly wounded
- the boarding route being compromised
- an overwhelming display of power
- successful intimidation
- another reasonable player action

---

## Suggested Performance

Someone looks around the deck.

At the wounded.

At the party.

At the rapidly collapsing raid.

Then:

> "BACK!"

Nobody immediately understands.

Louder:

> "BACK TO THE SHIP!"

The scavengers disengage.

Some run across the gangplank.

Others grab ropes.

Several drag wounded companions with them.

Someone begins cutting the grappling lines.

---

## Player Choice

The party may:

- allow them to retreat
- continue attacking
- pursue them
- board their vessel
- attempt to capture them
- demand surrender
- negotiate
- do something unexpected

Let the world respond naturally.

---

# Beat 7 - Aftermath

## Purpose

Give the battle consequences without immediately turning it into another quest.

---

## Scene

The scavenger vessel pulls away.

The sounds of combat fade.

For the first time since the party reached the deck...

there is quiet.

The endless Void remains around them.

The Current continues carrying the passenger vessel towards Meridian.

---

## Suggested Narration

> "The last grappling line falls away."

> "The other vessel begins to drift back."

[Pause]

> "For several seconds..."

> "...nobody says anything."

Then the ship comes alive again.

Crew rush toward damaged railings.

Guards check the wounded.

Passengers cautiously emerge.

Thomas appears from below.

His first question is not about the scavengers.

> "Anyone hurt?"

---

## Thomas

Thomas helps stabilise anyone who needs assistance.

If the party protected civilians or showed restraint, he notices.

He does not make a speech.

A simple:

> "Thank you."

is enough.

---

# Beat 8 - Port Meridian

## Purpose

Return to the original Session 1 structure.

The battle should now naturally explain why the party attracts official attention.

---

## Scene

The passenger vessel continues along the Current.

Port Meridian eventually appears ahead.

The players now have time to properly observe the world around them.

The Captain speaks with Thomas about the attack.

The players may overhear pieces of the conversation.

No immediate reward is offered.

---

## Arrival

Once docked, the Captain reports the raid.

Thomas confirms what happened.

The authorities quickly learn that several of the newest Voidmarked helped defend the vessel.

After registration, a guard approaches the group.

> "You six."

A glance between them.

> "Governor wants to see you."

If asked why:

> "Thomas says you were useful on the way in."

The guard shrugs.

> "Apparently that got someone's attention."

---

# Transition To Session Flow

Continue with the Governor sequence.

The Governor may acknowledge the incident briefly:

> "Thomas tells me you were helpful on the way in."

He does not congratulate them excessively.

He does not recruit them because they won a fight.

Their actions merely explain why he believes they may be capable enough to hear what he has to say.

The conversation then proceeds normally.
