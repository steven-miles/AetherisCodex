# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Tactics

> "They came looking for valuables.
>
> Staying alive is considerably more valuable."

---

# Purpose

The scavengers are not professional soldiers.

They are desperate people who believed they had found an easy target.

Their tactics should reflect this.

At the beginning of the encounter they are:

- Confident.
- Loud.
- Intimidating.
- Disorganised.
- Expecting little resistance.

As the battle progresses they become:

- Surprised.
- Nervous.
- Defensive.
- Protective of one another.
- Increasingly interested in escape.

The encounter should visibly change as the scavengers realise who they have attacked.

---

# The Scavenger Plan

Before the party becomes involved, the scavengers' plan is simple:

1. Grapple the passenger vessel.
2. Establish a route between the ships.
3. Board quickly.
4. Intimidate the crew and passengers.
5. Search the newly arrived Voidmarked for valuables.
6. Take anything unusual.
7. Escape before Port Meridian can respond.

They expect fear to do most of the work.

They are not prepared for a serious fight.

---

# Combat Priorities

At the beginning of combat:

1. Control the deck.
2. Intimidate resistance.
3. Find valuables.
4. Protect the boarding route.
5. Avoid unnecessary casualties.

Once the battle turns against them:

1. Stay alive.
2. Protect injured scavengers.
3. Reach the scavenger vessel.
4. Preserve an escape route.
5. Leave.

Loot immediately stops mattering once retreat begins.

---

# Phase One - Confidence

At the beginning of the encounter, the scavengers still believe the raid will succeed.

They spread across the deck rather than fighting as a coordinated unit.

They shout demands such as:

> "Hands where we can see them!"

> "Nobody needs to get hurt!"

> "Valuables. Now."

> "Give us what you brought through!"

They may attempt to:

- Shove guards aside.
- Threaten passengers.
- Grab bags or unusual possessions.
- Block access to the gangplank.
- Move deeper onto the passenger vessel.

They do not immediately attack everyone they see.

Their goal is robbery through intimidation.

---

# Phase Two - Surprise

The first significant display of player power should change their behaviour.

Examples include:

- A powerful spell.
- A scavenger being dropped immediately.
- An impressive martial attack.
- An unexpected transformation.
- Several scavengers being defeated at once.
- A player crossing onto their ship.
- Any ability they have never encountered before.

Let them react.

Possible reactions:

> "What was THAT?!"

> "You said they were passengers!"

> "THEY ARE PASSENGERS!"

> "Since when do passengers do that?!"

The scavengers begin looking towards their ship.

They have not retreated yet.

But they are thinking about it.

---

# Phase Three - Survival

Once it becomes clear that the party is significantly more dangerous than expected, the scavengers stop trying to control the passenger vessel.

Their behaviour becomes defensive.

They:

- Move closer to escape routes.
- Protect wounded allies.
- Use Shove to create distance.
- Avoid isolated fights where possible.
- Stop attempting to steal possessions.
- Begin calling warnings to one another.
- Look towards the Boss for instructions.

The Boss should visibly realise that the raid has failed before giving the retreat order.

---

# Phase Four - Retreat

When retreat is called:

> "BACK!"

Then louder:

> "BACK TO THE SHIP!"

From this point onwards, the scavengers are no longer trying to win the encounter.

They are trying to escape it.

---

# Raider Tactics

Scavenger Raiders are simple combatants.

Before retreat they generally:

**Move → Attack or Shove → Hold position**

They prefer targets who are:

- Blocking their route.
- Protecting passengers.
- Preventing access to valuables.

They do not deliberately focus attacks on one player simply because doing so is mechanically optimal.

They are reacting to the fight around them.

---

## During Retreat

Raiders:

- Disengage where possible.
- Run towards the gangplank.
- Grab ropes.
- Help injured allies.
- Abandon stolen goods.
- Defend themselves only when necessary.

A Raider who cannot escape may surrender.

---

# Scrapper Tactics

Scrappers are the scavengers most willing to physically stand between danger and their companions.

Before retreat they:

- Hold the gangplank.
- Push opponents away from vulnerable scavengers.
- Protect the Boss.
- Create space for Raiders.

They prefer Shove when controlling space is more useful than dealing damage.

---

## During Retreat

Scrappers often leave last.

They:

- Cover retreating Raiders.
- Help wounded scavengers move.
- Use `Get Back!` to create distance.
- Retreat once others are clear.

This is not heroic discipline.

They simply do not want their friends killed.

---

# Boss Tactics

The Scavenger Boss begins the encounter believing they are in control.

They initially remain closer to the scavenger vessel.

They shout instructions across the boarding route and allow their people to do most of the work.

They may enter the passenger vessel once resistance becomes serious.

---

## Before Things Go Wrong

The Boss:

- Commands Raiders.
- Protects the boarding route.
- Attempts to intimidate resistance.
- Uses `Move!` to reposition scavengers.
- Personally fights only when necessary.

---

## When Things Go Wrong

The Boss becomes increasingly concerned with escape.

They:

- Stop caring about valuables.
- Begin pulling scavengers back.
- Keep the route to their vessel open.
- Use `Move!` to help allies escape.
- Order retreat before the entire group is destroyed.

The Boss is not willing to die for this raid.

---

# Retreat Trigger

Do not treat retreat as a mathematical requirement.

Call the retreat when it feels obvious that the scavengers know they have lost.

Useful triggers include:

- 4 scavengers defeated.
- The Boss falls below half hit points.
- The Boss is defeated.
- A devastating player ability changes the battle.
- The gangplank is destroyed or removed.
- Players successfully board the scavenger vessel.
- The scavenger vessel itself is threatened.
- Successful intimidation.
- Another player action makes continuing obviously foolish.

The DM may call retreat earlier.

---

# If The Boss Falls

If the Boss is reduced to 0 hit points:

The nearest conscious scavenger immediately calls:

> "We're done!"

> "GET OUT!"

The scavengers begin retreating.

They do not fight harder to avenge the Boss.

If possible, they attempt to take the Boss with them.

---

# If A Scavenger Falls

Nearby scavengers react.

They are not disposable enemies.

If the fallen scavenger can safely be recovered, another may attempt to:

- Drag them away.
- Stabilise them.
- Carry them towards the scavenger vessel.

This becomes increasingly common once retreat begins.

---

# If A Player Falls

The scavengers do not attack unconscious player characters.

They move on.

If a player is clearly dying and combat has ended, the scavengers will not prevent someone from helping them.

They came to steal.

Not murder.

---

# If A Civilian Is Threatened

The scavengers avoid deliberately harming civilians.

A frightened passenger is useful.

A dead passenger creates consequences they do not want.

They may:

- Threaten civilians.
- Grab them.
- Push them aside.
- Demand valuables.

They should not deliberately execute or seriously harm them.

---

# If Players Surrender

If the party unexpectedly surrenders, the scavengers accept.

They demand valuables.

They do not kill anyone.

The encounter can continue through negotiation, deception or another player plan.

---

# If Scavengers Surrender

A trapped scavenger may:

- Drop their weapon.
- Raise their hands.
- Immediately stop fighting.

They expect the party to understand surrender.

If questioned, they readily admit why they attacked.

Rumours reached them that people had appeared from other worlds carrying unusual possessions.

They thought they could make easy money.

---

# If Players Board The Scavenger Vessel

Allow it.

Do not artificially prevent the party from crossing.

The scavenger vessel contains additional scavengers who are:

- Operating the vessel.
- Managing the grappling lines.
- Preparing the escape.
- Watching the battle.

They are not automatically additional combatants.

If players board during the retreat, these scavengers panic and attempt to get the ship moving.

The encounter may naturally change into a chase, negotiation, surrender or capture.

Follow the players' choices.

---

# Gangplank

The gangplank is the easiest route between vessels.

If removed or destroyed:

- Scavengers rely on grappling ropes.
- Retreat becomes more chaotic.
- Some scavengers may surrender if trapped.

Do not prevent players from destroying it.

Actions have consequences.

---

# Grappling Ropes

Several heavy ropes connect the vessels.

Players may:

- Swing from them.
- Climb them.
- Cut them.
- Pull on them.
- Use them creatively.

Scavengers use the same options.

During retreat, scavengers begin cutting lines as soon as their companions are clear.

---

# Falling Into The Void

Neither side casually attempts to throw people into the Void.

Everyone understands how dangerous that is.

Shoves should normally be used to:

- Create space.
- Move someone away from the gangplank.
- Break a defensive position.
- Escape.

If player choices create a situation where someone could fall, resolve it naturally.

Do not artificially protect enemies from consequences.

Do not artificially punish players either.

---

# Passenger Guards

The guards should remain largely cinematic.

Use them to show that combat is occurring elsewhere without adding unnecessary turns to initiative.

Examples:

> A guard catches a scavenger's blade against their shield.

> Two guards struggle to force another boarder back towards the gangplank.

> A wounded guard drags themselves towards the hatch.

If a player helps them, allow the situation to change.

The guards support the encounter.

They do not solve it.

---

# Captain Vex Sterling

Captain Vex Sterling remains focused on keeping the passenger vessel safely within the Current.

Vex does not abandon the controls to join the battle unless absolutely necessary.

During combat, Vex may shout practical warnings:

> "Keep them away from the engine!"

> "Watch those lines!"

> "Don't cut that one unless you want us drifting!"

Vex understands the ship better than anyone aboard.

Use Vex primarily to communicate immediate dangers the players would reasonably not understand yet.

---

# Player Creativity

This encounter deliberately contains more possibilities than rules.

Players may attempt things that are not covered in this document.

When that happens:

1. Decide whether it is physically possible.
2. Determine whether a roll is necessary.
3. Choose an appropriate ability or skill.
4. Set a reasonable DC.
5. Describe the consequence.

General guidance:

**DC 10** — straightforward under pressure

**DC 12** — requires some skill

**DC 15** — difficult or risky

**DC 18** — exceptional

If there is no meaningful consequence for failure:

Do not ask for a roll.

---

# DM Reminder

The goal is not to make the scavengers tactically perfect.

The goal is to make them believable.

They expected easy prey.

They found six Level 5 adventurers.

Let them make mistakes.

Let them become frightened.

Let them retreat.

And most importantly:

Let the players discover what their characters can do.
