# Encounter 001 - Raid on the Current
## Consequences

> "Aetheris does not judge every choice.
>
> But people remember what they see."

---

# Purpose

The Raid on the Current is the party's first opportunity to affect the world around them.

Their actions influence:

- Captain Vex Sterling's report.
- Thomas's first impression of the party.
- The Governor's first impression of the party.
- The passengers who witnessed the attack.
- The surviving scavengers.
- The party's earliest reputation in Port Meridian.

These consequences should be remembered.

They should not become arbitrary rewards or punishments.

---

# Important Principle

The Governor already has a reason to speak with the newly arrived Voidmarked.

The missing island exists regardless of what happens during this encounter.

The raid does not unlock the Chronicle.

Instead, it changes the circumstances surrounding the party's arrival.

Never require a specific outcome for the story to continue.

---

# Outcome - Decisive Victory

If the party immediately helps and successfully drives the scavengers away:

Captain Vex's report describes them as:

- Capable.
- Calm under pressure.
- Willing to protect others.
- Significant contributors to the defence of the vessel.

Thomas hears this report upon their arrival.

The Governor later receives the same information.

This creates a strong first impression.

---

# Outcome - Victory With Mercy

If the party defeats the scavengers while allowing them to retreat or accepting surrender:

The crew notice.

The guards notice.

Captain Vex notices.

The party has demonstrated that they are both capable and restrained.

This should create the strongest positive impression.

Captain Vex's report makes particular mention of their restraint.

Thomas respects it.

The Governor takes note.

---

# Outcome - Excessive Violence

If the party continues attacking scavengers who are clearly retreating:

The mood changes.

The crew become uncomfortable.

Captain Vex still acknowledges that the party defended the vessel, but the report reflects what happened.

The party may notice:

- Guards watching them more carefully.
- Passengers keeping their distance.
- Less warmth during the aftermath.
- Thomas becoming more cautious during their arrival.

The Governor still speaks with them.

He simply begins the conversation knowing that these newcomers may be dangerous.

---

# Outcome - Killing Surrendered Scavengers

If the party deliberately kills scavengers who have clearly surrendered:

This is serious.

The guards intervene if reasonably able.

Captain Vex's report explicitly mentions what happened.

Upon reaching Port Meridian:

- The party is treated cautiously.
- Thomas remains professional but guarded.
- Authorities may ask additional questions.
- Weapons may be watched closely.
- The Governor knows what occurred before meeting them.

Do not immediately turn the campaign into a criminal prosecution unless the circumstances justify it.

But do not ignore the action.

Aetheris remembers.

---

# Outcome - The Party Refuses To Fight

The players are never required to participate.

If they remain below deck:

The guards and crew eventually drive the scavengers away.

The vessel may suffer additional damage.

Some possessions may be stolen.

The party reaches Port Meridian safely.

Captain Vex has no particular reason to mention them in the report.

Thomas meets them as ordinary new arrivals.

The Governor may still eventually speak with the Voidmarked group because of the wider situation.

The story continues.

The players simply arrive without having established themselves through the raid.

---

# Outcome - Protecting Civilians

Players may choose to remain below deck specifically to protect passengers rather than joining the main battle.

This is not the same as doing nothing.

If scavengers reach the hold or civilians are endangered, those actions matter.

Captain Vex and the crew recognise that the party helped protect lives.

Their contribution should be included in the report.

---

# Outcome - The Party Struggles

If the battle goes badly:

Do not escalate simply because the encounter was expected to be easy.

The passenger guards continue fighting.

The crew protect civilians.

The scavengers remain interested in valuables rather than killing people.

Possible developments include:

- Scavengers taking loot and retreating.
- Captain Vex ordering defensive measures.
- Guards helping create an escape.
- The scavengers deciding they have taken enough.
- Negotiation.

Failure does not end the Chronicle.

It changes the story.

---

# Outcome - The Scavengers Win

If the scavengers successfully overwhelm resistance:

They do not slaughter the passengers.

They take what valuables they can quickly carry.

This may include possessions belonging to the player characters if the circumstances allow it.

Then they leave.

The party arrives in Port Meridian having experienced their first defeat in Aetheris.

Captain Vex reports what happened.

The Governor may still become interested in the new arrivals.

The stolen possessions may create future opportunities if the players decide they want them back.

Do not automatically create a recovery quest.

Let the players decide whether the loss matters to them.

---

# Outcome - Scavengers Captured

If the party captures some of the scavengers:

The prisoners are handed to Port Meridian's authorities upon arrival unless the party takes another action.

The scavengers can explain:

- They heard about the new Voidmarked.
- They believed objects from other worlds could be valuable.
- They expected frightened passengers.
- They wanted an easy robbery.

They possess no secret knowledge about the missing island.

They are not connected to a larger conspiracy.

Their story really is that simple.

---

# Outcome - The Scavenger Vessel Is Captured

If the party prevents the scavenger vessel from escaping:

Allow it.

Upon arrival near Port Meridian, the situation becomes an administrative problem.

The vessel was used during an attempted robbery.

Port authorities will want to inspect it.

Captain Vex can confirm how it came into the party's possession.

Possible outcomes include:

- Temporary confiscation as evidence.
- Legal salvage proceedings.
- Return to a legitimate previous owner if one exists.
- Sale after investigation.
- Another reasonable legal outcome.

Do not invent a rule purely to prevent the party from owning it.

However, capturing a criminal vessel does not automatically make it legally theirs.

---

# Outcome - Players Pursue The Scavengers

If the party boards the scavenger vessel and refuses to allow it to escape:

Follow the players.

The encounter may become:

- A surrender.
- A capture.
- A short pursuit.
- A negotiation.
- A second confrontation.

Do not force the players back onto the passenger vessel because the written encounter expects them there.

Captain Vex will prioritise the passenger vessel and will not recklessly abandon the Current to follow them.

Player choices determine what happens next.

---

# Captain Vex Sterling

Captain Vex's report should reflect what actually happened.

Do not decide the report before the encounter.

Possible descriptions include:

## Heroic

> "They protected my passengers when they had no reason to."

## Capable

> "They can handle themselves."

## Restrained

> "Could've killed the lot. Didn't."

## Concerning

> "They helped us. I'm glad they were on our side."

## Uninvolved

> "Didn't see much from them. Can't blame them. They'd only just woken up."

Vex does not exaggerate.

The report is practical and factual.

---

# Thomas

Thomas first meets the party when the vessel reaches Port Meridian.

His first impression is influenced by:

- The condition of the ship.
- Captain Vex's report.
- The behaviour of the party after docking.
- How they treat injured passengers and crew.
- Whether prisoners arrive with them.

Thomas remains professional regardless.

His warmth may change.

---

# The Governor

The Governor receives information through the normal chain of authority.

He should not know every detail immediately.

What reaches him is the important summary.

Examples:

> Six newly arrived Voidmarked helped repel a raid.

Or:

> Six newly arrived Voidmarked displayed unusual combat capability.

Or:

> New arrivals involved in violent incident aboard incoming passenger vessel.

Or:

> Nothing noteworthy.

The Governor's existing concerns remain unchanged.

The report simply influences his first impression of the party.

---

# Surviving Scavengers

If scavengers escape:

They remember the party.

This does not automatically create a recurring enemy.

Most will simply remember the day they made an extremely poor decision.

Stories may spread through the parts of Meridian where scavengers operate.

The details will probably become less accurate with every retelling.

Do not force this into a future plot.

Allow it to exist in the world.

---

# Passengers

The passenger vessel carries witnesses.

Some will remember the party.

Depending on what happened, stories may begin circulating:

- New Voidmarked fought off scavengers.
- Someone used extraordinary magic.
- Someone crossed between moving ships.
- Someone saved a passenger.
- Someone showed mercy.
- Someone did something frightening.

These should remain small rumours.

The party does not become famous from one fight.

---

# Reputation

Do not use numerical reputation points.

Record impressions instead.

Possible early reputations include:

**Unknown**

**Helpful**

**Capable**

**Brave**

**Merciful**

**Reckless**

**Dangerous**

**Unpredictable**

Different people may hold different impressions simultaneously.

There is no universal reputation score.

# Final Principle

The purpose of consequences is not to punish players for making unexpected choices.

It is to respect those choices.

If they show mercy:

Remember it.

If they show cruelty:

Remember it.

If they fail:

Remember it.

If they do something nobody expected:

Remember that too.

The world continues from whatever actually happened.
