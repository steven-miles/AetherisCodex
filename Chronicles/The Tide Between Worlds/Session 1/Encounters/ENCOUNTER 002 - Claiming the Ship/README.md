# Encounter 002 – Claiming the Ship

> "The objective is not to defeat them.
>
> The objective is to leave with a ship."

---

# Purpose

This encounter builds upon the combat philosophy introduced during the Raid on the Current.

The players have already experienced a straightforward threat.

This encounter asks a different question:

**What happens when the people standing in your way aren't villains?**

The objective is not to defeat the Driftwrights.

The objective is to acquire the Sea Bird.

Combat is one possible solution.

Negotiation, intimidation, persuasion, stealth, bribery and clever thinking are equally valid.

The encounter should reinforce that conflict in Aetheris does not always have clearly defined heroes and enemies.

---

# The Situation

The Driftwrights possess an old merchant vessel:

**The Sea Bird.**

It is not abandoned.

It is not worthless.

And it is not simply waiting for the players to take it.

The Driftwrights have invested time, labour and materials into keeping the vessel alive.

Arthur considers every ship under his care worth protecting.

The Governor's token gives the party a reason to be heard.

It does not give them ownership.

The players must decide how they approach the problem.

---

# Encounter Pillars

- Ownership has value.
- The Driftwrights are people, not obstacles.
- Intelligent people protect what they believe is theirs.
- Violence has consequences.
- Surrender is normal.
- Negotiation remains possible even after initiative begins.
- The environment should matter.
- Clever solutions should be rewarded.
- The Sea Bird should feel earned.

---

# Arthur

Arthur is not waiting for a fight.

He is the owner of the Driftwrights and has spent his life working among them.

He listens before judging.

He recognises the Governor's token.

That earns the party his attention.

It does not automatically earn them the Sea Bird.

Arthur wants to understand:

- Who they are.
- Why they need the ship.
- What they intend to do with it.
- Whether they can be trusted with something his people have worked to preserve.

If the conversation becomes heated, Arthur becomes serious.

If violence begins, his priority is protecting people.

Not winning.

---

# Success

The players acquire the Sea Bird.

How they achieve that is entirely their decision.

Possible outcomes include:

- Arthur willingly gives them the ship.
- The party negotiates an agreement.
- They offer something valuable in exchange.
- They earn the Driftwrights' respect.
- They convince Arthur their journey is worth supporting.
- They find an unexpected solution.
- They take the ship by force.

All of these are valid outcomes.

They do not all have the same consequences.

---

# Failure

Failure should never end the Chronicle.

If the players fail to acquire the Sea Bird, another opportunity should naturally emerge.

They might:

- Repair the relationship with Arthur.
- Find another vessel.
- Earn passage elsewhere.
- Steal something later.
- Discover an entirely different solution.

Failure changes the story.

It does not stop it.

---

# If Combat Begins

Combat should feel fundamentally different from the Raid on the Current.

The scavengers attacked the party.

The Driftwrights are defending their home, livelihood and people.

They:

- Protect one another.
- Protect Arthur.
- Protect the Sea Bird.
- Avoid killing whenever possible.
- Accept surrender.
- Attempt to stabilise the injured once the danger has passed.
- Stop fighting when continuing would achieve nothing.

The players should understand through behaviour alone:

**These people are not their enemies unless the party chooses to make them enemies.**

---

# Consequences

How the party behaves here matters.

Arthur and the Driftwrights remember:

- Respect.
- Mercy.
- Honesty.
- Cleverness.
- Violence.
- Damage to ships.
- Attacking surrendered people.
- Protecting injured workers.
- Keeping promises.

The Sea Bird may leave the warehouse regardless.

The relationship the party leaves behind is another matter entirely.

---

# Design Notes

Encounter 001 gives the players a chance to discover what their characters can do.

Encounter 002 gives them a chance to decide **who those characters are**.

The Raid on the Current presents an obvious threat.

Claiming the Sea Bird presents people with understandable reasons for opposing the party.

There is no intended solution.

Do not steer the players towards negotiation.

Do not steer them towards combat.

Present Arthur, the Driftwrights and the situation honestly.

Then let the players decide what happens.

The important outcome is not simply:

**The party got a ship.**

It is:

**The party got a ship, and Aetheris remembers how they did it.**
