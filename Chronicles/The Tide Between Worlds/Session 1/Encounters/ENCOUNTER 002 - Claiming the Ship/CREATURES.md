# Encounter 002 – Claiming the Ship
## Creatures

> The Driftwrights are not monsters.
>
> They are workers defending what belongs to them.

---

# Encounter Balance

Designed for:

**6 Level 5 Characters**

Combat is:

**Optional**

This encounter is not designed around defeating every Driftwright.

If combat occurs, the purpose is to create a believable conflict between the party and a group of people protecting their workplace, their ships and one another.

The Driftwrights:

- Do not fight to the death.
- Do not attempt to kill the player characters.
- Accept surrender.
- Protect injured allies.
- Stabilise injured people once it is safe.
- Stop fighting when continuing no longer serves a purpose.

The encounter should remain recoverable through conversation even after violence begins.

---

# Running The Driftwrights

Do not put every Driftwright in the warehouse into initiative.

Around **15 Driftwrights** may be present in or around the warehouse.

Most are:

- Working.
- Watching.
- Moving equipment.
- Protecting ships.
- Staying out of the fight.
- Getting help if necessary.

Only use enough active combatants to represent the immediate confrontation.

Recommended active combatants:

- Arthur
- 4 Driftwright Workers
- 2 Driftwright Veterans

This is not necessarily the number who must fight.

If only one player throws a punch, the entire warehouse does not instantly draw weapons.

Let the conflict escalate naturally.

---

# Driftwright Worker

*Medium Humanoid*

A working member of the Driftwrights.

Most are shipwrights, salvagers, mechanics and labourers rather than trained warriors.

They know how to handle themselves.

They would rather get back to work.

---

**Armor Class:** 13

**Hit Points:** 24

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 (+2) | 12 (+1) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) | 12 (+1) | 10 (+0) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +4, Investigation +2

**Tool Proficiencies:** Carpenter's Tools or Tinker's Tools

**Passive Perception:** 11

---

## Actions

### Work Hammer

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 6 (1d8 + 2) bludgeoning damage.

The hammer is a tool first.

It just happens to also hurt.

---

### Grab Them

The Worker attempts to grapple a creature within 5 feet.

**Athletics:** +4

Workers prefer restraining aggressive characters over seriously injuring them where possible.

---

## Behaviour

Workers:

- Defend themselves.
- Protect nearby Driftwrights.
- Attempt to restrain isolated attackers.
- Move injured people away from danger.
- Avoid unnecessary damage to the warehouse.
- Avoid damaging ships.

If the fighting stops:

They stop.

---

# Driftwright Veteran

*Medium Humanoid*

Some Driftwrights have been doing dangerous work for decades.

A number are former sailors.

Some were once pirates.

They are older now.

That does not mean they have forgotten how to fight.

---

**Armor Class:** 15

**Hit Points:** 42

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 (+3) | 13 (+1) | 15 (+2) | 11 (+0) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +5, Perception +4, Insight +4

**Passive Perception:** 14

---

## Actions

### Multiattack

The Veteran makes two attacks with their Work Hammer.

### Work Hammer

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 7 (1d8 + 3) bludgeoning damage.

---

### Put Them Down

Instead of dealing damage with a successful Work Hammer attack, the Veteran may attempt to knock the target prone.

The target must succeed on a:

**DC 13 Strength or Dexterity saving throw**

or fall prone.

The target chooses the ability used.

---

## Reaction

### Not The Ship

When a creature within 5 feet attempts to deliberately damage a ship or important piece of workshop equipment, the Veteran may move up to 5 feet and make one Work Hammer attack against that creature.

This movement does not provoke opportunity attacks.

---

## Behaviour

Veterans are more disciplined than ordinary Workers.

They:

- Protect younger Driftwrights.
- Guard access to the ships.
- Attempt to isolate dangerous characters.
- Use positioning rather than reckless aggression.
- Take threats to the vessels seriously.

They do not pursue fleeing characters unless there is an immediate reason.

---

# Arthur

*Medium Humanoid*

Arthur owns and leads the Driftwrights.

He is in his mid-forties.

He began working with the Driftwrights as a child and spent his life working his way through the organisation.

The founders are gone.

The Driftwrights are his responsibility now.

The workers call him:

**Chief.**

Arthur is not a legendary warrior.

He is an experienced working man who has spent decades around dangerous ships, rough sailors and people who occasionally solve disagreements with their fists.

---

**Armor Class:** 15

**Hit Points:** 55

**Speed:** 30 ft.

---

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 (+3) | 12 (+1) | 16 (+3) | 13 (+1) | 15 (+2) | 15 (+2) |

---

**Skills:** Athletics +5, Insight +4, Intimidation +4, Persuasion +4, Perception +4

**Tool Proficiencies:** Carpenter's Tools, Tinker's Tools

**Passive Perception:** 14

---

# Arthur's Actions

## Work Hammer

*Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.

**Hit:** 7 (1d8 + 3) bludgeoning damage.

Arthur carries the same sort of tools as everyone else.

He does not carry a ceremonial weapon.

---

## That's Enough

Arthur chooses one creature within 5 feet.

The target must succeed on a:

**DC 13 Strength or Dexterity saving throw**

or be grappled by Arthur.

Arthur generally uses this against someone he believes can still be reasoned with.

While grappling them, he may say something like:

> "You finished?"

---

## Get Them Out

Arthur chooses one willing Driftwright within 30 feet who can hear him.

That Driftwright may immediately move up to half their speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

Arthur primarily uses this to:

- Move injured workers.
- Clear people from danger.
- Prevent someone becoming trapped.
- De-escalate the fight.

---

# Arthur's Reaction

## Not In My Yard

When a creature Arthur can see deliberately attacks:

- A surrendered person.
- An unconscious person.
- A non-combatant.

Arthur may immediately move up to half his speed towards that creature.

This movement does not provoke opportunity attacks.

If he reaches them, Arthur may make one Work Hammer attack.

At this point, Arthur is no longer laughing.

---

# Arthur's Behaviour

Arthur does not begin combat unless circumstances genuinely justify it.

If violence starts, he initially attempts to understand what is happening.

His priorities are:

1. Protect people.
2. Stop the fight.
3. Protect the ships.
4. Prevent unnecessary damage.
5. Get law enforcement involved if necessary.

Arthur does not need to prove that he is the strongest person in the warehouse.

He already has everyone's respect.

---

# Arthur's Tone During Combat

Arthur can remain surprisingly conversational during minor violence.

A fist fight or harmless scuffle may even amuse him.

> "That your best?"

> "Come on, lad."

> "I've been hit harder by loose timber."

If things become genuinely dangerous:

The humour disappears.

Immediately.

> "Enough."

> "Put it down."

> "Nobody dies in my yard."

This tonal change should tell the players that they have crossed a line.

---

# The Watching Driftwrights

Approximately 15 Driftwrights may be present around the warehouse.

Not all participate.

Some:

- Watch.
- Shout encouragement.
- Laugh at a harmless scuffle.
- Move tools out of the way.
- Protect the Sea Bird.
- Move injured workers.
- Fetch Arthur.
- Run for law enforcement.

Their reaction depends on what the players do.

---

# Escalation

Do not begin with seven combatants surrounding the party.

Escalate naturally.

## Minor Scuffle

If a player starts a fist fight or shoves someone:

Only the people immediately involved respond.

Other Driftwrights may laugh.

Arthur may allow it briefly.

---

## Serious Fight

If weapons are drawn or dangerous magic is used:

Nearby Driftwrights stop treating the situation as entertainment.

Workers move away.

Veterans intervene.

Arthur attempts to stop the fight.

---

## Lethal Threat

If someone:

- Attempts to kill a Driftwright.
- Attacks an unconscious person.
- Attacks someone who surrendered.
- Deliberately causes catastrophic ship damage.
- Creates an obvious threat to everyone in the warehouse.

The Driftwrights treat the party as genuinely dangerous.

Someone goes for law enforcement.

Arthur becomes completely serious.

---

# Unconscious Characters

The Driftwrights do not attack unconscious characters.

If a player character falls:

They are no longer considered an immediate threat.

If possible, a Driftwright moves them away from the fighting.

Once it is safe, the Driftwrights stabilise them.

They do the same for their own people.

---

# Surrender

The Driftwrights accept surrender immediately.

A surrendered character:

- Is not attacked.
- Is not mocked.
- Is not deliberately humiliated.
- May be disarmed while the situation is resolved.

Arthur's priority becomes ending the conflict and determining what happens next.

---

# Driftwright Defeat

If enough Driftwrights are injured that they can no longer reasonably protect the warehouse:

They surrender.

Arthur may personally call it.

> "Enough."

> "We're done."

The Driftwrights do not throw their lives away for the Sea Bird.

If the party takes the ship by force:

They have succeeded.

The consequences come afterwards.

---

# Player Victory

Do not require every active Driftwright to reach 0 hit points.

Combat can end through:

- Surrender.
- Negotiation.
- Intimidation.
- Arthur deciding enough is enough.
- The party backing down.
- The Driftwrights being overwhelmed.
- Law enforcement arriving.
- Another reasonable outcome.

The objective remains:

**Acquire the Sea Bird.**

Not:

**Defeat the Driftwrights.**

---

# Healing

The Driftwrights keep basic medical supplies in the warehouse.

After combat, they stabilise injured people where reasonably possible.

This includes player characters.

Their willingness to provide further assistance depends on what happened.

Someone who punched a Worker and then backed down may still receive help.

Someone who deliberately murdered a surrendered Driftwright should expect a very different response.

---

# Ships

The Driftwrights care deeply about the vessels in their yard.

Damaging one deliberately causes a stronger reaction than breaking ordinary workshop equipment.

The Sea Bird is not indestructible.

If players damage it:

Record the damage.

They may still acquire the ship.

They will simply inherit the damage they caused.

---

# Law Enforcement

The Driftwrights are underworld-adjacent.

They are not enemies of Port Meridian's authorities.

The law understands what the Driftwrights do and generally appreciates that someone handles the work nobody else wants.

If serious violence occurs:

Someone eventually goes for law enforcement.

Do not use this as an artificial combat timer.

It is simply the natural response to escalating violence in a functioning city.

---

# DM Reminder

The Driftwrights have statistics because the players may choose violence.

The statistics do not mean violence is expected.

Arthur is not a boss.

The Workers are not minions.

The Veterans are not guards waiting for initiative.

They are people at work.

If combat happens:

Let them behave like people.

If combat stops:

Let them stop fighting.

And if somebody gets hurt:

They make sure they don't die.
