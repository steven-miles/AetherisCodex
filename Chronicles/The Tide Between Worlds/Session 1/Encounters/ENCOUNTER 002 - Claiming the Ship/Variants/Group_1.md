# Encounter 002 - Claiming the Ship
## Group 1 Variant

> The Sea Bird is worth protecting.
>
> The Driftwrights are willing to prove it.

---

# Party

**Party Size:** 6

**Character Level:** 5

**Combat:** Optional

---

# Purpose

This configuration preserves the original combat assumptions for Encounter 002.

The Driftwrights are not intended to defeat the party.

They are intended to:

- Protect Arthur.
- Protect one another.
- Protect the Sea Bird.
- Restrain reckless attackers.
- Prevent unnecessary damage.
- Stop once the conflict no longer serves a purpose.

The encounter should remain recoverable through conversation even after initiative begins.

---

# Creature Statistics

Use the standard statistics from:

`../CREATURES.md`

No statistical changes are required.

---

# Active Combatants

If the conflict escalates into a full confrontation, use up to:

- Arthur
- 4 Driftwright Workers
- 2 Driftwright Veterans

**7 potential active combatants**

This is a maximum working configuration.

Do not place all seven into combat automatically.

---

# Escalation

The Driftwrights respond proportionally.

## Minor Aggression

Examples:

- One player shoves someone.
- A weapon is drawn but not used.
- A single punch is thrown.
- Someone attempts to force their way past a worker.

Response:

- 1–2 Workers intervene.
- Arthur attempts to de-escalate.
- Veterans watch but do not immediately join.

---

## Serious Violence

Examples:

- A Driftwright is badly injured.
- Weapons are used.
- The Sea Bird is deliberately damaged.
- Arthur is attacked.
- Multiple party members begin fighting.

Response:

Add:

- Additional Workers.
- 1 Veteran.

Arthur becomes more direct.

---

## Full Escalation

Examples:

- The party attempts to seize the Sea Bird by force.
- Multiple Driftwrights are incapacitated.
- The party continues attacking after warnings.
- The warehouse or ships are placed in serious danger.

Response:

The full active group may become involved:

- Arthur
- 4 Workers
- 2 Veterans

Even here:

The Driftwrights are trying to end the threat.

Not kill the party.

---

# Arthur

Use Arthur's standard statistics.

Arthur should prioritise:

1. De-escalation.
2. Grappling or restraining aggressive characters.
3. Protecting injured Driftwrights.
4. Protecting ships.
5. Ending the fight.

Arthur does not need to maximise damage simply because initiative has begun.

---

# Workers

Workers should:

- Grapple.
- Block routes.
- Protect one another.
- Pull injured people away.
- Avoid surrounding one player unless that character is an immediate danger.

They should behave like workers defending their workplace.

Not trained soldiers executing battlefield tactics.

---

# Veterans

Veterans represent the Driftwrights' most capable defenders.

They should enter combat only when the situation clearly justifies it.

Their role is primarily to:

- Stop dangerous attackers.
- Guard the Sea Bird.
- Protect Arthur.
- Control movement.
- End serious escalation quickly.

---

# Surrender

If the party surrenders:

The Driftwrights stop immediately.

They:

- Take weapons if necessary.
- Stabilise injured characters.
- Move people away from danger.
- Attempt to understand what happens next.

Combat does not need to continue simply because initiative was rolled.

---

# Driftwright Surrender

The Driftwrights may also stop fighting.

This becomes appropriate when:

- Arthur orders them to stop.
- Continued fighting would cause unnecessary deaths.
- The party clearly controls the situation.
- The Sea Bird is already lost.
- A negotiated solution becomes possible.

---

# If The Party Wins Cleanly

A decisive victory does not automatically make the Driftwrights enemies forever.

If the party:

- Avoids killing.
- Accepts surrender.
- Protects injured workers.
- Limits damage.
- Shows restraint.

Arthur may grudgingly respect them.

The Driftwrights may eventually treat the confrontation as a brutal but understandable dispute.

---

# If The Party Behaves Cruelly

Record:

- Killing surrendered Driftwrights.
- Attacking unconscious people.
- Deliberately destroying ships.
- Unnecessary violence.
- Threats against civilians or workers.

These consequences should extend beyond the immediate encounter.

---

# Expected Experience

Group 1 should feel capable of winning a physical confrontation.

The important question is not:

**Can they beat the Driftwrights?**

It is:

**What are they willing to do to get the Sea Bird?**
