# Encounter 002 – Map Design

> "The Driftwright Warehouse is a working shipyard first and an encounter location second."

---

# Design Intent

The warehouse should feel alive.

Nothing exists because combat requires it.

Everything exists because the Driftwrights genuinely need it to repair and launch ships.

If combat occurs, the environment naturally becomes part of the encounter.

---

# Overall Layout

The warehouse is built on the edge of the Void.

Visitors enter from the city side and naturally progress towards the launch platform.

The layout follows the same journey every ship does:

Street
↓

Reception
↓

Workshop

↓

Repair

↓

Launch

↓

The Void

---

# Approximate Dimensions

Overall Warehouse

Approximately 45m long

Approximately 25m wide

High vaulted ceiling approximately 12m

Large enough to comfortably house two sizeable ships.

---

# Zone 1 – Entrance

## Purpose

Receiving visitors.

Paperwork.

Initial meetings.

Storage of contracts and manifests.

## Features

- Large timber entrance doors
- Foreman's office
- Visitor waiting area
- Notice board
- Hanging Driftwright emblem
- Famous wooden figurehead mounted high on the wall

## Atmosphere

Quiet.

Professional.

Smells faintly of timber before the workshop opens beyond.

---

# Zone 2 – Salvage Yard

## Purpose

Breaking down vessels beyond repair.

Recovering usable materials.

## Features

- Hull ribs of a much larger dismantled vessel
- Stacked reclaimed planks
- Bundled masts
- Engine components awaiting inspection
- Large labelled storage racks

## Gameplay

Provides:

- Heavy cover
- Climbing opportunities
- Difficult terrain
- Improvised weapons

Players immediately understand:

Not every ship can be saved.

---

# Zone 3 – Main Workshop

## Purpose

Heart of the Driftwright operation.

The busiest area.

## Features

- Multiple workbenches
- Tool wall
- Forge
- Rope storage
- Timber racks
- Engine hoist
- Overhead travelling crane
- Suspended cargo

Workers constantly move through this area carrying equipment.

## Gameplay

Environmental interaction includes:

- Cutting suspended loads
- Swinging on chains
- Moving heavy cargo
- Creating temporary cover
- Blocking movement

---

# Zone 4 – The Sea Bird

## Purpose

Primary focus of the encounter.

## Current State

The Sea Bird is nearing completion.

The magical engine has been removed.

The hull rests on heavy timber repair blocks.

Scaffolding surrounds both sides.

Several hull planks have recently been replaced.

Fresh varnish can still be smelled on parts of the railings.

## Gameplay

- Multi-level combat
- Interior exploration
- Climbing
- Jumping between scaffolding
- Narrow walkways
- Cover from the hull

---

# Zone 5 – Launch Platform

## Purpose

Preparing repaired ships for departure.

## Features

- Heavy mooring chains
- Guide rails
- Engine alignment frame
- Launch Bell
- Wide open view across the Void

Once the engine is reinstalled the ship slowly becomes weightless before being guided from the platform.

## Tradition

Every departing ship rings the Launch Bell once before leaving.

---

# Typical Workers

Approximately fifteen Driftwrights work throughout the warehouse on a normal day.

Only a small number become involved if violence breaks out.

Most continue working, observe from a distance or attempt to calm the situation.

The warehouse should never feel empty simply because combat begins.

---

# Design Notes

The Driftwright Warehouse is intended to become a recurring location throughout the Chronicle.

Players should feel comfortable returning here.

It should be remembered as the birthplace of the Sea Bird rather than simply the location of its first encounter.

When illustrating the map, prioritise authenticity over symmetry.

The warehouse should feel like it has grown over decades of practical use.

Messy in places.

Efficient everywhere.

Lived in.
