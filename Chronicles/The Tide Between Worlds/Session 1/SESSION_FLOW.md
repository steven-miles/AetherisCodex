# Session 1 - Session Flow

> This document combines storyboard and director notes.
>
> It is not a rigid script.
>
> Improvise freely while protecting the intent of each beat.

---

# Beat 1 — Awakening

## Director's Intent

Introduce confusion, safety and curiosity.

The players should feel that something impossible has happened, but the people around them treat it as an ordinary morning.

---

## Emotional Journey

Confusion → Safety → Curiosity

---

## Scene

The players have been unconscious for roughly twelve hours, though they do not know this.

They wake in the dim hold of a passenger vessel near Port Meridian.

Around twenty people of all ages are packed inside.

Nobody was chosen.

The arrivals are random.

The vessel smells of timber, food, rum and an unfamiliar scent from the magical engine.

Faint rays of morning light pass through gaps in the hull.

---

## Suggested Performance

> "The first thing you become aware of... is not light."

[Pause]

> "It's sound."

[Pause]

> "The slow groan of timber."

> "Ropes creaking somewhere overhead."

> "A bell rings in the distance."

[Pause]

> "Then another."

> "Muffled voices."

> "Too quiet to understand."

[Pause]

> "Beneath everything..."

[Pause]

> "A steady rhythmic hum."

> "Constant."

> "Unfamiliar."

> "As though the ship itself is breathing."

[Pause]

> "Your body feels heavy."

> "Your thoughts come slowly."

> "As your senses return, you realise you're lying on rough wooden boards."

[Pause]

> "One by one..."

> "You open your eyes."

Allow the players to react.

Above:

> "They're coming around."

Another voice:

> "Good."

> "We'll be docking shortly."

A hatch opens.

Bright light spills in.

A uniformed dock escort waits while people adjust.

He counts everyone.

> "Morning."

[Pause]

> "Watch your footing coming up."

---

## Resident Focus

### Player View

A dock escort.

### DM View

Thomas.

Only reveal his name if asked.

Patient.

Efficient.

Kind.

---

## Voidmarks

Do not announce that the players have Voidmarks.

Let them discover the marks themselves.

If a player discovers theirs, ask:

> "Where is your mark?"

Let the player define it.

---

## Transition

Thomas leads the group toward the deck.

---

# Beat 2 — First Steps

## Director's Intent

Reveal Aetheris through discovery.

Guide attention naturally rather than telling players what to notice.

---

## Emotional Journey

Curiosity → Disbelief → Wonder → Overwhelm → Belonging

---

## Scene

The gangplank is old oak.

The dock is clean but worn.

Bright orange clamps hold ships in place.

Colourful national flags break up the otherwise practical harbour.

The city is huge.

If players look toward the Voidsea, only a few distant island silhouettes are visible.

Important places remain beyond the horizon.

---

## Suggested Performance

> "The morning sun catches your eyes as you step out onto the deck."

[Pause]

> "Above you..."

> "Blue sky."

> "Clouds drifting lazily overhead."

> "For just a moment..."

> "Everything feels almost normal."

Let them react.

The harbour is loud:

bells, gull-like birds, laughter, hammers, ropes, engines and conversation.

Then a deep harbour bell rings.

A nearby ship begins to depart.

Thomas raises a hand.

> "Hold a moment."

Children wave.

Parents wave too.

Crew salute the engine.

The ship catches the Current.

If the players follow it with their eyes:

> "...and beyond the ship..."

[Pause]

> "...there is no sea."

[Pause]

> "Only an endless black Void stretching beneath the world."

Stop speaking.

Let them react.

Then:

> "Governor's expecting you."

---

## Registration

Clerks ask:

> "Name?"

Then:

> "Place of origin?"

If the answer is unknown to Aetheris:

> "Unknown."

No judgement.

Just paperwork.

---

## Edith

A clerk.

Ink-stained.

Busy.

Thomas:

> "Busy?"

Edith:

> "Busier."

Then both continue working.

---

## Transition

The group is guided toward the Governor.

---

# Beat 3 — The Governor

## Director's Intent

Provide clarity without removing mystery.

The Governor shares a burden, not a quest.

---

## Emotional Journey

Overwhelm → Relief → Concern → Purpose

---

## Scene

A practical office.

Ledgers.

Maps.

Reports.

Cold tea.

The Governor is late fifties, well dressed but not extravagant, visibly exhausted.

---

## Suggested Performance

The Governor does not look up immediately.

He raises one finger.

Finishes his paperwork.

Then:

> "Please."

> "Take a seat."

[Pause]

> "I imagine you have questions."

Let the players drive.

Useful phrases:

> "We don't know."

> "Our best understanding..."

When the conversation slows, he goes to a map.

> "There is something I haven't told the public."

[Pause]

> "Because I can't."

He points.

> "There used to be an island here."

[Pause]

> "Three days ago..."

> "...a ship returned."

> "The island did not."

If asked why it is secret:

> "If merchants believe islands are disappearing..."

[Pause]

> "...those ships stop sailing."

> "If those ships stop sailing..."

> "...every island pays the price."

He needs information.

He cannot send a major official expedition without attracting attention.

The players are already looking for answers.

Their interests align.

> "If I were standing where you are..."

[Pause]

> "...it's where I would begin looking."

---

## Governor's Token

The group receives a simple wooden token with the Governor's mark.

It signals trust and permission.

It opens doors.

It does not make them famous.

---

## Transition

The Governor returns to work.

A hand gesture sends someone else to escort the group out.

For the first time, nobody is telling them where to go.

---

# Beat 4 — Loose Ends

## Director's Intent

Give the players genuine freedom.

This is where they stop being passengers and start making decisions.

---

## Scene

They emerge into the administrative building and then into Meridian.

They have:

- the Governor's token
- money or belongings from their home realms
- temporary barracks
- the missing island's location
- no obvious way to investigate it yet

The temporary barracks contain other Voidmarked from previous days.

This is where the party can naturally separate from the larger group.

---

## Possible Directions

### Tavern

Quiet, comfortable, low conversation, cards, charts and captains.

### Barracks

Other Voidmarked.

### Docks

Ships and captains.

### Markets

Trade, including possible interest in strange foreign currency.

### Travellers' Quarter

Tents, temporary stalls and the Fortune Teller.

---

## Fortune Teller

The Governor does not mention her.

The city does.

Someone may casually say:

> "You could always ask the old woman."

Her tent sits among travellers.

It does not scream "fortune teller".

She has simply always been there.

---

## Transition

Beat 4 ends when the players begin actively working out how to reach the missing island.

---

# Beat 5 — Finding a Way Forward

## Director's Intent

Let the players discover that reaching the island is possible, but difficult.

Do not tell them they need their own ship.

Let them conclude it.

---

## Emotional Journey

Hope → Frustration → Discovery → Determination

---

## Tavern Gossip

The Navigators Guild has published a newly discovered route that cuts a journey between two islands by around twenty percent.

This is the big public news.

The world has its own priorities.

---

## Famous Captain

The first captain they notice is extravagant, confident, respected and famous.

Exactly what a new arrival thinks a captain should be.

They are not available.

They are aspiration.

---

## Refusal

An older captain may refuse them.

> "No."

Or:

> "I'm not taking strangers."

Or:

> "Not to an island that isn't there anymore."

Or:

> "My crew would walk."

The issue is risk.

---

## Joshua

Joshua moves cargo around the docks.

Friendly.

Hard-working.

Dreams of becoming a navigator.

More specifically, he wants people to sail a Current because he discovered it.

He can point the players toward possible ships.

A mundane first meeting is best.

Maybe a cart catches.

Maybe crates slip.

If helped:

> "Cheers."

If asked about ships:

> "Depends what sort of ship you're after."

---

## Paths Toward a Ship

Depending on where the players go:

- spare old merchant ship
- scavenger-held vessel
- gang-controlled ship
- owner willing to trade it for money, work or a favour

Possible solutions:

money, persuasion, work, favours, intimidation, theft, negotiation or combat.

---

## Underworld

Do not undermine Meridian's safety.

The underworld is a layer beneath the city, not its true face.

Someone may say:

> "If you're looking for a ship nobody will miss..."

> "...try below."

---

## Transition

Beat 5 ends when the players identify a realistic ship they might acquire.

---

# Beat 6 — Claiming the Ship

## Director's Intent

Give the players ownership.

This beat is about earning a ship, not winning a fight.

---

## Ship

An old merchant vessel.

Nothing glamorous.

Cracked hull.

Repairable.

The players can upgrade it over the Chronicle until it becomes special because of them.

---

## History

Long ago, the ship belonged to a pirate who was then a nobody.

They later became legendary.

They lost this ship early in their career while gambling.

Do not let that history overshadow the players.

---

## Current Ownership

Could be:

- scavengers
- a gang
- a legitimate owner who considers it spare

---

## Combat

If combat happens, it is over ownership.

The opponents are not cartoon villains.

Non-lethal solutions are valid.

---

## Non-Combat Solutions

- buy it
- convince the owner
- promise work
- trade a favour
- negotiate
- sneak
- steal
- earn respect

Do not punish clever solutions.

---

## Naming

The ship already has a name.

Renaming ships is considered bad luck.

The players may rename it anyway.

Let sailors react.

Do not lecture them.

---

## Becoming a Crew

If they fight together for the ship, they may feel like a crew immediately.

If they acquire it peacefully, that identity may come later.

Let it be earned.

---

## Transition

The ship is theirs.

The hull is repaired enough for a first voyage.

---

# Beat 7 — First Departure

## Director's Intent

End Session 1 with possibility.

The players should finish excited for the next island.

---

## Preparing to Leave

A small temporary crew helps them safely leave Meridian.

Possible helpers include hired sailors or smugglers whose respect they earned.

Every player should have something to do.

---

## Thomas

Thomas may be on the dock.

He checks something practical.

> "Looks good."

Then returns to work.

---

## Joshua

Joshua comes to see them off.

He tells one of the players:

> "Captain rings it."

Now the party has to decide who the captain is.

---

## First Argument

Most likely:

> "Who's steering?"

Lean into it.

---

## Departure

The bell rings.

People pause.

Joshua waves.

The clamps release.

The ship moves away.

---

## First Sailing Lesson

The first thirty minutes are about work.

Navigation.

Lines.

Engine.

Wheel.

Lookout.

Everyone helps.

---

## Rookie Captain Mistake

Rookie captains often forget to slow before engaging the magical anchor.

Instead of a smooth stop:

SCREECH.

A violent halt.

Experienced sailors know the sound immediately.

Keep this as a recurring joke.

---

## Entering the Current

After around thirty minutes, they reach the Current.

The ship settles into the flow.

For the first time, they understand what it means to "catch" a Current.

---

## Final Image

Port Meridian shrinks behind them.

Thomas is already back at work.

Edith is processing someone else.

The Governor is still buried in paperwork.

Joshua is a small figure on the dock.

Ahead, a distant island silhouette appears.

They do not reach it.

---

## Possible Final Line

> "For the first time since arriving in Aetheris..."

[Pause]

> "...the horizon belongs to you."

End Session 1.
