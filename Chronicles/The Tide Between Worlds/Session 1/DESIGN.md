# Session 1 Design

## Purpose

Session 1 exists to introduce Aetheris.

Not its history.

Not its politics.

Its feeling.

Players should leave the session wanting to discover what lies beyond the next horizon.

---

# Session Beats

## Beat 1 — Awakening

**Emotion:** Confusion.

The players awaken aboard a passenger vessel with other Voidmarked.

They are disoriented.

The crew are calm.

The players understand almost nothing about where they are.

---

## Beat 2 — Raid on the Current

**Emotion:** Shock and action.

The passenger vessel is attacked by desperate scavengers.

The players are given an opportunity to help but are not forced to fight.

This beat exists to let the players test their Level 5 characters immediately.

During the raid, they see the Void for the first time.

Combat should establish that:

- enemies value their lives
- surrender and retreat are normal
- the environment matters
- the players are free to solve problems creatively
- victory does not require killing everyone

The raid also gives Captain Vex Sterling reason to report the party's actions upon arrival in Port Meridian.

---

## Beat 3 — First Steps

**Emotion:** Wonder and overwhelm.

The players arrive at Port Meridian.

They meet Thomas for the first time on the docks.

The city does not stop because they arrived.

Their first experience of Meridian should show:

- order
- trade
- routine
- movement
- ships
- everyday life around the impossible

The Void is no longer a shocking reveal.

Here, it is simply part of the world.

---

## Beat 4 — The Governor

**Emotion:** Concern and purpose.

Captain Vex's report gives Port Meridian's authorities reason to recognise the party as unusually capable new arrivals.

This explains why the Governor takes an interest in them.

The raid does not create the Governor's problem.

He already has one.

The Governor reveals that an island has disappeared and that he has kept it quiet to protect trade.

He gives them a direction, not an order.

---

## Beat 5 — Loose Ends

**Emotion:** Freedom.

The players are released into Meridian with:

- temporary accommodation
- belongings or currency from their home worlds
- a Governor's token
- the missing island's location

For the first time, nobody is guiding them.

They may:

- explore
- gather rumours
- visit the tavern
- visit the barracks
- search the docks
- explore the markets
- seek the Fortune Teller
- do something unexpected

---

## Beat 6 — Finding a Way Forward

**Emotion:** Possibility and determination.

The players work out how they might reach the missing island.

They may gather rumours, meet captains, seek information or explore the city.

The idea that they may need their own ship should emerge naturally.

Joshua provides a friendly connection to the docks and can point the party towards the Driftwrights.

---

## Beat 7 — Claiming the Sea Bird

**Emotion:** Ownership.

The players encounter the Driftwrights and attempt to acquire the Sea Bird.

The important challenge is not combat.

It is earning ownership.

Possible approaches include:

- negotiation
- money
- work
- promises
- trust
- persuasion
- intimidation
- theft
- combat
- something unexpected

Combat remains possible.

It is not required.

The Driftwrights should feel like people before they ever feel like opponents.

Arthur should feel like someone whose respect matters.

---

## Beat 8 — First Departure

**Emotion:** Adventure.

The players leave Meridian aboard the Sea Bird.

They begin learning to sail.

They experience the Launch Bell.

They catch their first Current.

They see a distant island ahead.

The session ends before they reach it.

---

# Discoveries

By the end of Session 1, players should have discovered:

- This is not their world.
- They are not alone.
- They are capable.
- The Void is real.
- Aetheris is beautiful.
- The world does not revolve around them.
- Something is wrong.
- The Governor does not have all the answers.
- Trade holds Aetheris together.
- Attacks on major Currents are unusual.
- The missing island is secret.
- Skyships represent freedom and possibility.
- Enemies do not necessarily fight to the death.
- Problems can be solved in multiple ways.
- Actions affect how people see them.
- Adventure waits beyond the horizon.

---

# Mysteries Introduced

- Why are people arriving from other worlds?
- What are the Voidmarks?
- Why did an island disappear?
- Are the disappearances connected to the Voidmarked?
- What lies beneath the Void?
- Why does the Fortune Teller already know about them?
- Can the players ever return home?

---

# Design Rules

## Show first. Explain later.

The first view of the Void occurs during the raid.

Do not stop the scene to explain it.

Let the players experience it before they understand it.

---

## Curiosity before exposition.

The players should discover information because they ask questions or interact with the world.

Avoid lore lectures.

---

## Let the players test their characters.

The raid exists partly because the players want an early combat encounter.

Let them use their abilities.

Let powerful actions feel powerful.

The encounter does not need to last a specific number of rounds.

A short, spectacular victory is still a successful encounter.

---

## Combatants are people.

The scavengers do not exist to die.

They attack because they are desperate.

They retreat because they want to live.

The Driftwrights fight only if the players create that situation.

Every conflict should have a reason.

---

## The world feels alive.

Port Meridian has its own priorities.

Ships arrive.

Ships depart.

People work.

Trade continues.

The players are important to their own story.

They are not automatically important to everyone else.

---

## The players are not heroes. Not yet.

Defending the passenger vessel may earn respect.

It does not make them famous.

One good action does not transform them into legendary figures.

Let reputation grow naturally.

---

## Reputation comes from witnesses.

Captain Vex reports what actually happened.

Thomas forms his own impression.

The Governor receives an administrative summary.

Passengers tell stories.

Surviving scavengers remember.

There is no universal reputation score.

Different people may believe different things about the party.

---

## Design problems, not solutions.

The raid is a problem.

The missing island is a problem.

Reaching it is a problem.

Acquiring the Sea Bird is a problem.

Do not decide how the players solve them.

Combat is one possible answer.

Negotiation, money, work, stealth, surrender, creativity and unexpected choices are equally valid.

---

## Never require a specific player choice for the Chronicle to continue.

If the players refuse to fight:

The story continues.

If they lose:

The story continues.

If they never speak to the expected NPC:

The world provides another route.

If they solve a problem in a way the documents did not anticipate:

Follow them.

---

## Consequences are memory, not punishment.

If the players show mercy:

Remember it.

If they behave recklessly:

Remember it.

If they protect someone:

Remember it.

If they do something frightening:

Remember it.

The purpose of consequences is to make the world respond honestly.

Not to push the players towards a preferred style of play.

---

## The Sea Bird should be earned emotionally.

The Sea Bird is not merely Session 1 loot.

It should become:

- a home
- a record of the party's adventures
- something they improve
- something they care about

The Launch Bell is the emotional moment when ownership becomes real.

---

## End with possibility.

Do not finish Session 1 with answers.

Finish with a horizon.

The party should understand enough about Aetheris to want more.

---

# End Goal

Players should finish Session 1 excited about the adventure ahead and asking:

> "Where are we going next?"

A successful Session 1 should leave them feeling:

- curious about Aetheris
- comfortable experimenting with their characters
- attached to the Sea Bird
- interested in returning to Port Meridian
- eager to reach the next island
