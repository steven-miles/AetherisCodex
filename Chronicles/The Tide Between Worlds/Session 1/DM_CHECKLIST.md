# Session 1 - DM Checklist

> "Players remember how they felt, not what they rolled."

Use this checklist before Session 1.

---

## Before You Begin

Aetheris is not a story you tell.

It is a world you present.

Your job is not to guide the players to the "correct" outcome.

Your job is to understand the world well enough that it responds naturally to whatever the players choose.

Don't rush moments.

Don't fear silence.

Trust your players to be curious.

Trust the world to respond honestly.

Whenever you're unsure what should happen next, ask yourself one question:

> **"What would naturally happen in Aetheris?"**

If you answer that honestly...

You'll almost always make the right decision.

# Refresh Locations

## Passenger Vessel

- [ ] Remember the players wake below deck.

The opening should feel:

**Confusing → Safe → Curious**

Do not explain Aetheris too quickly.

The players should barely begin orienting themselves before the raid interrupts them.

---

## The Void

- [ ] Remember the first true Void reveal happens during the raid.

Do not reveal it casually before then.

When they reach the deck:

Give the image room to land.

Then let combat interrupt their ability to process it.

After the raid:

Give them time to actually look.

---

## Port Meridian

- [ ] Remember the city should overwhelm.

Busy.

Lived in.

Functional.

The Void that shocked the party moments earlier is ordinary here.

Ships arrive.

Ships leave.

People work.

The city does not stop because the players arrived.

---

## Driftwright Warehouse

- [ ] Remember this is a workplace.

Not an arena.

Everything has a purpose.

The Sea Bird is the visual centrepiece.

Arthur does not appear immediately.

## Encounter 001 Reminders

- [ ] This combat is designed for 6 Level 5 characters.
- [ ] Target difficulty is easy.
- [ ] Let the players test their abilities.
- [ ] Do not extend combat just because it ends quickly.
- [ ] Scavengers are desperate, not professional pirates.
- [ ] They expect intimidation to work.
- [ ] They retreat once they realise they have lost.
- [ ] They do not attack unconscious characters.
- [ ] They do not deliberately slaughter civilians.
- [ ] Allow surrender.
- [ ] Allow the party to board the scavenger vessel.
- [ ] Allow the vessel to be captured if the players genuinely achieve it.
- [ ] Use ropes, gangplank, rigging and both ships as opportunities.
- [ ] Do not prescribe environmental solutions.

---

## Raid Reputation

After Encounter 001, know:

- [ ] What Captain Vex saw.
- [ ] What Captain Vex reports.
- [ ] Whether the party showed mercy.
- [ ] Whether they protected civilians.
- [ ] Whether they used excessive violence.
- [ ] Whether any scavengers were captured.
- [ ] Whether the scavenger vessel was captured.

This information affects:

- Thomas's first impression.
- The administrative report.
- The Governor's first impression.

It does not decide whether the Chronicle continues.

# At the Table

## Beat 1 — Awakening

- [ ] Introduce the world slowly.
- [ ] Allow silence.
- [ ] Let players investigate.
- [ ] Let them discover their Voidmarks.
- [ ] Do not introduce Thomas.
- [ ] Do not reveal the Void yet.
- [ ] End the calm with the impact against the ship.

---

## Beat 2 — Raid on the Current

- [ ] Ask for help rather than ordering the party to fight.
- [ ] Reveal the Void when they reach the deck.
- [ ] Roll initiative.
- [ ] Keep enemy turns fast.
- [ ] Let powerful abilities feel powerful.
- [ ] Let the scavengers become visibly afraid.
- [ ] End with retreat when the raid has clearly failed.
- [ ] Give the party time to process the Void afterwards.

---

## Beat 3 — First Steps

- [ ] Introduce Thomas on the dock.
- [ ] Let Captain Vex report the raid naturally.
- [ ] Show orange docking clamps.
- [ ] Show national flags.
- [ ] Make Meridian noisy and busy.
- [ ] Run registration.
- [ ] Introduce Edith.
- [ ] Let the Governor summons arise from the normal administrative process.

---

## Beat 4 — The Governor

- [ ] Let him finish paperwork before looking up.
- [ ] Let the players ask questions.
- [ ] Say "We don't know" when appropriate.
- [ ] Do not over-explain the world.
- [ ] Reveal the missing island only once the conversation naturally reaches it.
- [ ] Keep trade as the reason for secrecy.
- [ ] Give the Governor's token.
- [ ] Return the Governor immediately to work afterwards.

---

## Beat 5 — Loose Ends

- [ ] Let the party choose where to go.
- [ ] Do not force the tavern.
- [ ] Do not force the Fortune Teller.
- [ ] Remember the temporary barracks.
- [ ] Remember foreign money and objects may interest locals.
- [ ] Let Meridian have unrelated gossip and priorities.

---

## Beat 6 — Finding a Way Forward

- [ ] Do not tell them they need their own ship.
- [ ] Let captain refusals establish the difficulty.
- [ ] Introduce Joshua naturally.
- [ ] Let Joshua point towards the Driftwrights if appropriate.
- [ ] Keep the route flexible if the players find another sensible lead.

---

## Beat 7 — Claiming the Sea Bird

- [ ] Use Encounter 002.
- [ ] Let the party meet the Driftwrights before Arthur.
- [ ] Let Arthur enter naturally.
- [ ] Let the players explain themselves.
- [ ] Allow negotiation to carry the encounter.
- [ ] Do not force combat simply because combat stats exist.
- [ ] Let the outcome reflect their behaviour.

---

## Beat 8 — First Departure

- [ ] Give everyone a job aboard the Sea Bird.
- [ ] Let the players decide who acts as Captain.
- [ ] Let the Captain ring the bell.
- [ ] Allow the Launch Bell moment to breathe.
- [ ] Let Joshua wave them off.
- [ ] Use the rookie-anchor joke only if it feels natural.
- [ ] Let them catch their first Current.
- [ ] End before they reach the distant island.

---

# During NPC Conversations

- [ ] Listen more than you speak.
- [ ] Let players ask questions.
- [ ] Allow pauses.
- [ ] Reward curiosity.
- [ ] Don't force prepared dialogue if the conversation goes somewhere better.

---

# During Combat

- [ ] Intelligent creatures protect themselves.
- [ ] Most people surrender or retreat before dying.
- [ ] The environment matters.
- [ ] Violence is one solution.
- [ ] Never the only solution.
- [ ] Do not make enemies tactically perfect.
- [ ] Let NPC behaviour reflect who they are.

---

# Themes To Remember

- [ ] Wonder and danger can exist together.
- [ ] Curiosity before exposition.
- [ ] Ordinary people make extraordinary places.
- [ ] The world does not revolve around the party.
- [ ] Ships are homes, not equipment.
- [ ] Actions have lasting consequences.
- [ ] Enemies value their lives.
- [ ] Problems should have multiple solutions.
- [ ] Every encounter should create one memorable moment.

---

# Session Goal

By the end of Session 1, the players should feel:

- [ ] Curious about Aetheris.
- [ ] Comfortable using their characters.
- [ ] Attached to the Sea Bird.
- [ ] Interested in returning to Port Meridian.
- [ ] Excited to discover what lies beyond the horizon.

If those are true:

The session was a success.

---

# Final Reminder

You are not telling the players a story.

You are introducing them to a world.

Trust them to decide what happens next.

> **The repository records what happened. It does not decide what happens.**

---

# After the Session

Take ten minutes before changing any documents.

Ask yourself:

- What surprised me?
- What surprised the players?
- Which NPC did they enjoy most?
- Which moment made everyone smile?
- Did the raid feel fun?
- Did the players get to test their characters?
- Did the Void reveal land?
- Did the Driftwright encounter feel different enough from the raid?
- Did anything feel forced?
- What changed in the world because of the players?

Then review the Canon Changes sections in both encounters.

Only then begin updating the repository.
