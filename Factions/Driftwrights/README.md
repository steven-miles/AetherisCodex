# The Driftwrights

> *"Nothing built to sail should be left to rot."*

---

# Purpose

The Driftwrights reclaim, repair and repurpose ships that others have abandoned.

Where most people see a wreck...

The Driftwrights see another voyage.

They are not builders.

They are restorers.

Without them, Port Meridian would slowly disappear beneath forgotten hulls and abandoned dreams.

---

# What Everyone Knows

The Driftwrights work from an old warehouse dock on the edge of Port Meridian's industrial district.

If someone needs:

- an old mast,
- replacement planking,
- spare rigging,
- reclaimed engine parts,
- or a ship nobody else wants,

they know where to go.

The Driftwrights are respected for the quality of their work.

Even those who dislike them admit that they know ships better than almost anyone.

---

# What People Believe

People trust the Driftwrights...

...right until something goes missing.

Whenever rumours spread through the docks, someone eventually says:

> "Sounds like Driftwright work."

Whether they've done anything or not.

Many merchants quietly assume they have connections to smugglers.

Many smugglers assume they have connections to the Governor.

Neither side can prove it.

The Driftwrights never confirm or deny either rumour.

---

# What Nobody Knows

The Driftwrights were founded by a handful of retired pirates.

Not because they suddenly became noble...

Because they grew old.

After decades taking ships apart with cannon fire...

They discovered they enjoyed putting them back together far more.

Most people simply assume they've always existed.

---

# Philosophy

Every ship deserves another chance.

The Driftwrights don't believe ships have value because they're expensive.

They believe ships have value because somebody once trusted them enough to cross the Void.

To leave one to rot...

...is a waste.

---

# Their Code

Every Driftwright learns the same three rules.

> We don't kill.

> We don't steal.

> We finish the job.

Anyone unwilling to live by those rules is no longer welcome.

---

# Internal Conflict

The Driftwrights are changing.

The older members believe their purpose is simple.

Repair ships.

Earn an honest living.

Keep your head down.

The younger members dream of expanding beyond Port Meridian.

Some want to discover new islands.

Some want to build larger workshops.

Some dream of owning fleets rather than repairing them.

Neither side is wrong.

They're simply looking in different directions.

---

# Headquarters

The Driftwright warehouse sits beside a quiet warehouse dock, well away from the busy commercial harbour.

The first thing visitors notice is not the ships.

It's the sound.

Hammers.

Saws.

Ropes tightening.

Someone quietly singing while they work.

The smell of fresh timber, hot metal, tar and salt fills the air.

Nobody stops working simply because someone walks in.

Then...

Someone calls across the workshop.

> "Mind the plank."

Apparently visitors trip over it often.

Hanging above the central workbench is an old wooden figurehead.

Nobody remembers which ship it came from.

Whenever anyone asks...

A Driftwright simply shrugs.

> "Been here longer than us."

---

# Symbol

The Driftwrights mark their work with a simple emblem.

An anchor.

Surrounded by a shipwright's hammer and hand tools.

The mark is never displayed proudly on the outside of a ship.

Instead...

It is branded somewhere structural.

A hidden beam.

A supporting frame.

A repaired mast.

Not for recognition.

For pride.

If a ship is still sailing decades later...

A Driftwright knows exactly where to look.

---

# Relationship with Port Meridian

The city needs the Driftwrights.

The Driftwrights need the city.

Neither side particularly likes admitting it.

The Governor understands their importance.

The Harbour Master tolerates them.

Merchants rely on them.

Captains often pretend they don't.

---

# First Impression

Visitors expecting criminals are usually disappointed.

There are no dark corners.

No hidden blades.

No whispered conspiracies.

Just honest people doing difficult work.

Some are stripping old hulls.

Others are rebuilding them.

Someone carefully oils the handrail of an ageing merchant ship.

An older worker walks past.

Looks at the polished timber.

Grumbles.

> "Don't waste the good oil."

The younger worker smiles...

...and keeps polishing.

---

# Design Notes

The Driftwrights are not an enemy faction.

They are not an ally faction.

They are part of Port Meridian's ecosystem.

Players should feel comfortable returning here throughout the Chronicle.

This is where ships come to begin their second lives.
