# Port Meridian

> *"All skies lead to Meridian."* — An old captain's saying

---

# Overview

Port Meridian is the beating heart of Aetheris.

Situated upon one of the largest floating islands in the known world, it serves as the greatest centre of trade, diplomacy, exploration, and shipbuilding in existence. Every major trade route eventually passes through its docks, making it one of the few places where a farmer from a distant orchard island, a pirate captain from the Stormbreak Archipelago, and a scholar chasing forgotten legends might all share a drink in the same tavern.

To many, Port Meridian is the closest thing Aetheris has to a capital. While no single nation rules the skies, the city's influence reaches nearly every known island through its guilds, merchants, and shipping routes.

People come to Meridian seeking fortune.

Some find it.

Others disappear trying.

---

# The City

Port Meridian is built upon a vast floating island, its skyline dominated by towering docking spires capable of receiving hundreds of skyships at once. Beneath the city, enormous enchanted docking clamps extend from the island itself, locking vessels safely in place while cargo and passengers are unloaded.

The city never truly sleeps.

At any hour of the day, ships arrive carrying spices, livestock, enchanted artefacts, scholars, mercenaries, diplomats, explorers, and criminals. New arrivals are so common that few people pay them any attention.

If something exists somewhere in Aetheris, chances are it can be bought, sold, or stolen in Port Meridian.

---

# Districts

## The Grand Docks

The busiest harbour in the known world.

Hundreds of enchanted docking berths line the island's edge, where dockworkers, customs officials, merchants, sailors, and adventurers crowd the streets from dawn until long after sunset.

Most visitors see the Grand Docks before anything else.

---

## The Sky Market

The commercial heart of the city.

Food stalls stand beside magical artificers, wandering storytellers perform beside armourers, while traders loudly advertise exotic goods imported from distant islands.

Nearly anything can be purchased here—for the right price.

---

## The Gilded Ring

The wealthiest district.

Merchant princes, ambassadors, guild leaders, and respected nobles make their homes here.

The Governor's Hall overlooks immaculate streets filled with gardens, fountains, and expensive restaurants.

The city guard maintains a constant presence.

---

## The Foundries

A district of smoke, sparks, and industry.

Here, engineers build and repair skyships, artificers maintain arcane engines, and inventors constantly test new magical technology.

Every captain eventually visits the Foundries.

Usually with an expensive repair bill.

---

## The Underdock

Officially, it doesn't exist.

Hidden beneath the docks lies a maze of forgotten warehouses, abandoned ship berths, smugglers' tunnels, illegal markets, gambling dens, and secret taverns.

The city guard rarely ventures here without overwhelming numbers.

The criminal organisations that operate below have their own rules...

and they enforce them ruthlessly.

---

## Scholar's Rise

Built around Meridian's oldest observatory, this quieter district houses libraries, magical academies, mapmakers, historians, and navigators.

It is also home to one of the city's oldest residents.

A woman known only as...

**The Fortune Teller.**

---

# Government

Port Meridian is governed by a council representing the city's major guilds, with a Governor elected to oversee the city's interests and maintain peace between its many competing factions.

While the Governor holds considerable influence, no single person truly controls Meridian.

Trade does.

Every major decision is ultimately judged by one question:

*"Will this keep the city prosperous?"*

---

# Law

Meridian enforces surprisingly few laws.

Violence, murder, theft, and sabotage are punished harshly.

Smuggling, however...

exists in a curious grey area.

Everyone knows it happens.

Everyone claims to oppose it.

Yet somehow, every guild benefits from it.

---

# Daily Life

For most citizens, life revolves around the arrival of ships.

Dock bells mark the rhythm of each day.

Cargo arrives.

Markets swell.

Crews rotate.

News spreads.

By sunset, dozens of taverns overflow with stories gathered from every corner of the skies.

Many lifelong residents have never left Meridian.

Yet through the tales brought home by sailors, they feel as though they've travelled the whole world.

---

# Notable NPC

## The Fortune Teller

No one knows her real name.

Some say she has lived in Meridian for decades.

Others insist she has been there for centuries.

Every morning she opens a small tent tucked between Scholar's Rise and the Sky Market.

Every evening she closes it.

No one has ever seen where she goes.

She accepts only a single silver coin for a reading.

Sometimes she tells people exactly what they wish to hear.

Sometimes she warns them of futures they cannot escape.

Curiously, many captains refuse to begin long voyages without visiting her first.

Whether she possesses genuine prophetic gifts or merely understands people better than anyone else remains one of Port Meridian's enduring mysteries.

---

# Adventure Hooks

* A merchant vessel fails to arrive on schedule, carrying a cargo that could bankrupt an entire guild.
* An engineer believes someone is sabotaging arcane engines in the Foundries.
* Strange lights have been seen beneath the docks late at night.
* A famous pirate captain walks openly through the Sky Market under a temporary truce.
* A mapmaker offers a fortune for someone willing to chart an unexplored Arcane Current.
* The Fortune Teller refuses to read anyone's future for an entire week, claiming, "The cards cannot see beyond tomorrow."

---

# GM Notes

The Fortune Teller should never fully explain herself.

Whether she truly sees the future, subtly nudges people toward it, or simply understands fate better than anyone else should remain deliberately ambiguous.

She is intended to be a recurring figure throughout multiple campaigns, providing foreshadowing, mystery, and the occasional unsettling truth. Different characters may receive different readings, but her predictions should always come true—just rarely in the way anyone expects.

Port Meridian itself should evolve over time. Businesses change owners, districts recover from disasters, guilds gain and lose influence, and familiar faces grow older. Players should feel that the city remembers them long after their adventures take them elsewhere.
